# Memory hygiene

A personal AI is most useful when it remembers you — your goals, your schedule, your study plan. It becomes dangerous the moment it remembers your **patients**. Memory hygiene is the discipline of deciding, deliberately, what an AI may keep.

## What your AI may remember

- Your roles, goals, and current courses or rotations (the shape of your life, not its records).
- Your study progress: topics mastered, topics due, what the last commit-then-compare session exposed.
- Your preferences: how you like drafts structured, when you study best, what motivates you.
- Your projects and their 90-day wins.

## What must never enter it

- Anything about a patient — names, rooms, diagnoses, ages, memorable details, or "anonymized" stories that a classmate, family member, or auditor could reconstruct. De-identification is harder than it looks; the safe rule at your stage is **none**.
- Other people's private information: classmates' grades, instructors' remarks, family health details.
- Credentials — passwords, portal logins, badge numbers. An AI that remembers your password can be made to use it.

The test before saving anything: *if this memory synced to a vendor's server tonight and was breached tomorrow, who could be harmed?* If the answer includes anyone but you, it does not go in.

## The monthly review

Memory you cannot inspect is memory you cannot trust. Once a month, open your AI tools' memory or personalization settings and read what they hold. Delete what is stale, wrong, or should never have been there. If you find patient information, stop using the tool, record what you found **offline** — never retype identifiers into an AI chat — and follow your program's incident guidance. Prefer tools that keep memory local, visible, and deletable — files you own beat opaque vendor stores.

## Limitations

Tool memory interfaces change quickly; the routine here is tool-agnostic and may not match a specific product's settings. This guide covers your personal AI memory, not institutional systems — an employer's or school's AI tools have their own governance you do not control.
