# 04 — From Mess to Map

### Getting a Complicated Life Into a System You Can Actually Run

**Format:** 60 minutes · live or virtual · paper strongly preferred over screens
**Series:** Nurse AI OS — General Practice Workshops
**Boundary:** Non-clinical. No patient care, no medical advice, no PHI. Participants map
their own life and work commitments, not any workplace caseload.

---

## The promise

> **Setup:** You've been told you have a productivity problem. You've bought the apps. You've
> tried the system with the coloured tags.
>
> *(pause)*
>
> **Reveal:** You don't have a productivity problem. You have seventeen open loops living
> inside one head that was built to hold about four — and the fix isn't more discipline, it's
> getting them out of your head and onto a map.

**One sentence:** You will externalise everything you're currently carrying into a single
one-page map of your life and work, and leave with a workspace that can hold that map with
you instead of you holding it alone at 3 a.m.

---

## Who this is for

People running more than one life at once: a job, a household, a side project, an ageing
parent, a course, a small business. Shift workers whose weeks don't repeat. Nurses who manage
enormous complexity at work and come home to none of that structure. Founders holding a
company in working memory. Anyone whose honest answer to "what are you working on?" is a
sigh.

---

## Learning outcomes

By the end of this session, participants will be able to:

1. Perform a complete **open-loop inventory** across three spheres — personal, professional,
   community — without editing or judging during capture.
2. Sort every captured item into one of four categories: **active project · standing
   commitment · someday · not mine.**
3. Write a **project one-liner** containing owner, next physical action, and a done-condition
   they'd recognise if they tripped over it.
4. Identify at least one commitment to **kill or hand back**, and say the sentence that does it.
5. Describe what belongs on a **dashboard** and, more usefully, what does not.
6. Run a **weekly review** in twenty minutes using five fixed questions.

## Run of show (60 minutes)

| Time | Segment | What happens |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 0–5 | **Open: the dump** | Four minutes, silent, everything you are carrying. Every nagging thing. No editing, no ordering, no judging. The room gets very quiet and then the pens speed up. |
| 5–11 | **Teach: why loops cost more than tasks** | An unnamed obligation charges rent continuously. A named one charges once. Three spheres, and why mixing them is what makes a life feel unmanageable rather than merely full. |
| 11–16 | **⏸ DEMO PAUSE 1 — Raw mess in, sorted map out** | *See notes below.* |
| 16–28 | **Exercise: the four-way sort** | Every item goes into active project · standing commitment · someday · not mine. Hard rule: at least one item must move to *not mine* before the segment ends. Facilitator circulates and asks "whose is this, really?" |
| 28–33 | **⏸ DEMO PAUSE 2 — One item becomes a project record** | *See notes below.* |
| 33–41 | **Write: three project one-liners** | Top three active projects only. Each gets: what it's for · the next physical action · what done looks like · what it needs from me weekly · what it must never take from me. |
| 41–46 | **⏸ DEMO PAUSE 3 — The one screen** | *See notes below.* |
| 46–55 | **Design the weekly review** | Twenty minutes, same time each week, five questions. Participants pick the time and put it in the calendar in the room, out loud, before moving on. |
| 55–60 | **Close** | Reflection and call to action. |

## Demo pause notes

**⏸ DEMO PAUSE 1 — Raw mess in, sorted map out (≈5 min).**
Paste a genuinely messy, unpunctuated brain dump into the workspace and ask it to group,
de-duplicate, and — critically — ask you the questions it can't answer. Show the questions.
The reveal is that a good system doesn't guess at your life; it asks.
*Do not show:* anyone else's commitments. Use your own dump, mess and all. The mess is the
credibility.

**⏸ DEMO PAUSE 2 — One item becomes a project record (≈5 min).**
Take a single line — "sort out the website" — and turn it into a governed project record:
purpose, next action, done-condition, what it needs from you, and what it must never do
without asking. Say plainly: this is the moment a nagging feeling becomes a thing with edges.

**⏸ DEMO PAUSE 3 — The one screen (≈5 min).**
Show a local, private, one-screen dashboard. Then spend most of the demo on what is *not* on
it. A dashboard that shows everything is a second inbox. The discipline is exclusion.

---

## Practical takeaways (usable tonight)

- **A one-page map** of everything they are carrying, sorted four ways.
- **Three project one-liners** with real next actions, not verbs like "review" or "think about".
- **One thing killed or handed back**, and the exact sentence they'll use to do it.
- **The five weekly-review questions**: what moved · what's stuck and why · what's newly
  landed on me · what can go · what am I protecting this week.
- **A calendar entry** for the first weekly review, made in the room.

---

## Closing reflection and call to action

> Clarity is not a mood that arrives when things calm down. Things do not calm down.
>
> Clarity is a file you maintain. Twenty minutes a week, whether you feel like it or not,
> which is the same deal you already made with every other thing you take seriously.

**Call to action:** Run your first weekly review within seven days, at the time you just wrote
down. It will be uncomfortable and take thirty minutes instead of twenty. Do it anyway. The
second one takes fifteen.
