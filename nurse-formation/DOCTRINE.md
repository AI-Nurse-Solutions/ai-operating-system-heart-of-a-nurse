# NIN Nurse Formation Doctrine

## 1. Purpose

This doctrine governs how AI may participate in the education and
training of adult nurse learners: pre-licensure students, newly
licensed nurses in transition to practice, practicing nurses in
continuing development, and the educators, preceptors, and mentors who
form them.

Nursing education does not deliver content; it forms nurses. Formation
is the joint growth of three things — clinical judgment, skilled
know-how, and professional identity — and it is the only output that
matters. An AI that accelerates assignments while thinning judgment
has not helped education; it has consumed it.

The central claim:

> **AI in nursing education is governed by what it does to judgment. Any use that grows the learner's judgment is a candidate; any use that quietly replaces it is prohibited by design.**

This doctrine is not a tutoring product, a curriculum, a competency
scorer, a credentialing authority, an accreditation instrument, a
proctoring system, or a substitute for faculty, preceptors, boards of
nursing, or institutional policy.

## 2. Honest applicability

This doctrine distinguishes three states.

### 2.1 Binding design boundaries

Upon adoption, the following govern every formation proposal and
artifact:

- **No AI output may be the sole basis** for a grade, progression,
  remediation, professionalism, discipline, competency, entrustment,
  or admission decision;
- no fabricated clinical experience, simulated-hours, competency,
  readiness, or evaluation record;
- no PHI or reconstructable patient narrative in any learning
  environment — practice uses synthetic, patient-shaped data only;
- no covert monitoring of learners, and no secondary use of private
  reflections, prompts, drafts, or retrieval history for evaluation,
  ranking, or employment judgment;
- no answer-before-commitment default where formation is the declared
  purpose of the interaction;
- no undisclosed AI in the learner's loop — learners must know when AI
  participates in their formation, in what role, and with what limits;
- no claim of licensure success, clinical competence, or employment
  readiness attributed to AI use without an appropriate evaluation
  design.

### 2.2 Capability-conditional obligations

Formation-mode delivery, summative-intent gates, bias-sighting routes,
AI-assistance disclosure ledgers, and educator dashboards become
binding when those capabilities exist. They must not be represented as
operational before implementation and verification.

### 2.3 Formally activated programs

School adoption, curriculum integration, faculty development programs,
residency partnerships, continuing-education credit, competency
frameworks, and any institutional deployment require separate named
activation, authority, and evidence. This doctrine does not activate
them.

## 3. Governing maxims

> **Agents coach. Learners commit. Educators judge. Institutions credential. Nurses steward.**

> **The struggle is the curriculum. AI may scaffold the struggle; it must never perform it.**

> **Formation over efficiency: the product of nursing education is the nurse, not the assignment.**

> **AI never grades alone, and it never gates a career.**

> **Practice happens on patient-shaped data with no patients in it.**

> **Learner privacy is not a lesser privacy.**

## 4. Formation doctrine

### 4.1 What formation is

Formation braids three apprenticeships: knowledge (what a nurse must
understand), skilled know-how (what a nurse must be able to do), and
ethical comportment (who a nurse must become). Clinical judgment is
the spine that runs through all three. Two models anchor this doctrine
and any AI that serves it:

- **Tanner:** noticing, interpreting, responding, reflecting;
- **NCSBN Clinical Judgment Measurement Model:** recognize cues,
  analyze cues, prioritize hypotheses, generate solutions, take
  actions, evaluate outcomes.

Whatever AI touches in a curriculum, these are the muscles that must
bear load. An educational use of AI is evaluated by a single question:
after this interaction, did the learner's own noticing, interpreting,
and deciding get exercised — or bypassed?

### 4.2 The offloading hazard

The evidence base of 2024–2026 is consistent: unstructured reliance on
generative AI is associated with cognitive offloading and weakened
higher-order reasoning, and the youngest learners show the greatest
vulnerability to dependence. The same literature shows the counter-
pattern: structured, guided, reflective use — where the learner
commits, compares, and critiques — can strengthen critical thinking
rather than erode it.

The doctrinal consequence is a default posture, not a warning label:

- toward a learner, AI is **structured and effortful by design**, not
  frictionless by default;
- in formation contexts, friction is not a bug to remove but a dose to
  titrate — convenience is a cost to be justified, not a feature to
  maximize;
- answer-first delivery is an exception that requires a stated reason
  (reference lookup, accessibility, time-critical logistics), never
  the ambient default of a learning surface.

### 4.3 Adults own their learning

Every learner in scope is an adult. Andragogy therefore binds design:
adults are self-directing, bring experience that is itself curriculum
material, and learn best on real problems. AI may propose learning
pathways, surface gaps, and adapt pacing; the learner judges the
pathway, and disagreement with the machine's plan is a legitimate
learning act, not noncompliance. Personalization serves the learner's
goals — it is not a mechanism for institutions to steer, rank, or
surveil.

### 4.4 Where learning actually happens

Learning happens at the moments of commitment, error, feedback, and
reflection — not at the moment of receiving an answer. Simulation
doctrine follows: the debrief is the lesson. AI may build scenarios,
prepare debriefing questions, and organize observations; **a
consequential debrief is led by a human educator**, because the
debrief is where judgment is formed and where formation is judged.
Error must be free somewhere: the practice field exists so that the
cost of a wrong decision is a lesson, never a patient.

### 4.5 The formation quartet

No single support forms a nurse. Formation runs on four supports
together, and the doctrine treats them as complementary and
non-substitutable:

1. **The AI tutor** personalizes practice, adapts pacing, and keeps
   the **growth mirror** — a longitudinal picture of the learner's
   own judgment development that belongs to the learner. The mirror
   lives in the learner's private zone and is shared with a mentor by
   the learner's choice; it is never covert evaluation (§10).
2. **The human mentor** carries what cannot be written down: tacit
   know-how, practical wisdom, and the transfer of values. Judgment
   and identity are apprenticed from humans; no tutor replaces the
   mentor, and mentorship time is a formation outcome the AI exists
   to protect and enlarge (§11).
3. **The community** of peers and near-peers integrates learning:
   belonging, normalization of struggle, collaboration, and the
   professional identity that forms in company. AI may help a
   community coordinate; it must not simulate one.
4. **The proof of work.** The record of formation is work actually
   produced — deliverables, projects, case write-ups on synthetic
   data — held in the learner's portfolio with provenance and
   AI-assistance disclosure. Module completions and test scores
   annotate the record; they are not the record (§8).

A program missing one support does not compensate by strengthening
another: a better tutor does not replace the mentor, an active
community does not replace the mentor's accountability, and a rich
portfolio still requires human judgment of the work.

## 5. The five formation rules

### Rule 1 — First commitment

Where formation is the purpose, the learner's own read comes first.
The system elicits commitment — "what do you notice, what do you
expect, what would you do first" — before or alongside its answer,
and the comparison between the learner's read and the system's answer
is the teachable object. Students are coach-first by default; any
learner may opt in.

### Rule 2 — Visible reasoning

Answers arrive shaped for judgment: assumptions stated, alternatives
named, change conditions declared. The learner learns the shape of
judgment from the shape of the answer. A naked verdict teaches
deference; a visible chain of reasoning teaches appraisal.

### Rule 3 — The practice field

Practice happens on patient-shaped data with no patients in it.
Synthetic cases carry unmistakable generation markers, admission
boundaries reject anything that even resembles a real record, and
governance does not relax because data is synthetic — role gates,
action-mode caps, and human-authorization gates apply unchanged, so
the habits formed in practice are the habits governance expects in
the world.

### Rule 4 — Human evaluative authority

AI is formative by default and summative never alone. AI may draft
items, rubrics, and feedback for human review; it may score practice
work when the scoring is validated, disclosed, and appealable. But no
AI output may be the sole basis for a grade, progression, remediation,
professionalism, discipline, competency, or entrustment decision. The
educator's signature on a summative decision is a human act that
cannot be delegated to a machine, and every AI contribution to it must
be reviewable, overridable, and traceable.

### Rule 5 — The questioned tool

Challenging the AI is a competency, not a discourtesy. Learners are
taught to ask of every tool: *Who was in the training data? Who was
left out? How does it perform for learners and patients like mine?*
Mismatches between AI output and clinical judgment are bias sightings
and curriculum material. Overrides and near misses are learning
signals, not user failure — a formation environment that punishes
disagreement with the machine is forming the wrong nurse.

## 6. Role and authority doctrine

| Actor | May | May not |
|---|---|---|
| **Learner** | Direct their own learning, commit a first read, challenge outputs, keep private reflections private, disclose AI assistance, appeal AI-influenced decisions | Present AI work as their own thinking where disclosure is required, or access patient-specific recommendation behavior in student mode |
| **Educator** | Use AI to draft scenarios, items, rubrics, and feedback for their own review; judge and sign every summative decision; run debriefs; see disclosed learner work products | Delegate a summative decision to AI, view private learner reflections, or deploy an undisclosed AI in the learner's loop |
| **Preceptor / mentor** | Use AI to organize observations and prepare coaching conversations within their scope | Convert AI summaries into evaluation records without their own observation and judgment |
| **Community / peers** | Encourage, normalize struggle, review within competence, collaborate on shared work | Evaluate for progression, substitute for mentor accountability, or pressure disclosure of private reflections |
| **Program / institution** | Adopt tools behind governance gates, set local policy, authorize bounded pilots, own accountability for deployed AI | Claim competence, readiness, or licensure outcomes from AI use without an evaluation design; run covert monitoring; let procurement outrun governance |
| **AI (agents)** | Frame, probe, drill, draft, simulate, summarize what the learner may see, and propose pathways | Grade alone, determine progression, certify competence, lead a consequential debrief alone, monitor covertly, or claim teaching authority |
| **NIN / NAIO** | Steward the doctrine, literacy standards, review labels, and community formation pathways | Confer educational authority, accreditation, or competency status by publication |
| **Nurse AI OS** | Enforce the runtime boundaries in the embedding map and surface refusals as teachable moments | Substitute a runtime gate for an educator's judgment or an institution's policy |

Authority is scoped and human: a title grants nothing outside an
authenticated context, and no actor's AI use extends their authority
past what their role already holds.

## 7. AI literacy doctrine

AI literacy is a licensure-lifespan competency, not an orientation
module. Three literacies form the ladder, aligned with the NLN
foundational-to-advanced progression, the AACN *Essentials*
informatics competencies, and the state AI-literacy mandates now in
force:

1. **Foundational literacy** — what these systems are and are not;
   where they fail; how bias enters through data and design; privacy
   and disclosure duties; the difference between fluency and truth.
2. **Applied literacy** — governed use inside real workflows: writing
   prompts as professional documents, verification habits, escalation
   when output conflicts with assessment, reading provenance and
   review labels.
3. **Stewardship literacy** — participating in governance: reading
   audit trails, reporting bias sightings, sitting on review
   committees, shaping procurement and policy, holding a design seat.

Educators come first: **no program may require of learners a literacy
its faculty have not begun.** Faculty formation precedes learner
exposure in every adoption sequence.

## 8. Assessment and integrity doctrine

- **Formative uses are the default** and are allowed with disclosure:
  practice questions, feedback drafts, study pathways, self-assessment
  mirrors, spaced retrieval.
- **Summative uses are bounded:** AI may draft items and rubrics for
  human review and may assist scoring only where the assistance is
  validated for the population, disclosed to the learner, appealable,
  and never the sole basis of the decision.
- **Integrity is built on disclosure, not detection.** Programs prefer
  assessment designs where the thinking is visible — process evidence,
  oral defense, in-person demonstration, commit-then-compare artifacts
  — over product policing. AI-detection tools are unreliable and
  biased against some writer populations; **no AI-detector output may
  be the sole basis for a misconduct finding.**
- **The portfolio is proof of work.** Formation evidence is actual
  output — deliverables, projects, synthetic-case write-ups — carried
  in the learner's portfolio with provenance and AI-assistance
  disclosure per artifact. Scores and completions annotate the
  portfolio; they do not substitute for it.
- Academic integrity processes owe learners the same due process this
  doctrine demands for patients: evidence, explanation, appeal, and a
  human decision-maker.

## 9. Equity and bias doctrine

Bias work is a formation outcome, not a compliance annex.

- Every education AI adopted by a program must disclose known
  limitations and, where it evaluates or adapts to learners, provide
  performance evidence across relevant learner subgroups; "works on
  average" is not evidence of fairness.
- Learners and educators are taught the standard questions — *who was
  in the training data, who was left out, how does it perform for
  people like the ones in front of me* — and the standing habit of
  documenting mismatches between AI output and professional judgment
  as bias sightings.
- Bias sightings flow through a named route to governance review, and
  reporters see what happened to their reports.
- Language access and accessibility are equity requirements of the
  learning surface itself, not enhancements.

## 10. Learner data and privacy doctrine

Learner privacy is not a lesser privacy. The same architecture that
shields patients shields learners:

- learning records live in an explicit **educational_record** data
  zone; private reflections, journals, prompts, drafts, and retrieval
  history live in a **private** zone that faculty, manager, cohort,
  and executive views cannot reach;
- content never silently migrates between zones — a cross-zone move
  requires explicit, recorded approval;
- a learner's AI interactions are formative traces, not surveillance
  material: they must not feed grading, ranking, discipline,
  employment decisions, or analytics beyond disclosed,
  purpose-limited, aggregate-by-default uses;
- collection is minimal, retention is bounded, correction routes
  exist, and no business model may depend on selling or mining learner
  behavior.

The anti-surveillance doctrine of the [NIN Knowledge Commons](../knowledge-commons/DOCTRINE.md)
applies to formation data in full.

## 11. Educator formation doctrine

The educator is the accountable human in the loop, and the doctrine
protects the conditions that make that real:

- AI extends educator reach — drafting scenarios, triaging feedback,
  generating practice variation — and never replaces the educator
  relationship, which is itself a formative instrument;
- educator workload is a safety parameter: an education AI that adds
  documentation burden or review theater will be circumvented, and
  circumvention is a design failure, not a user failure;
- educators receive their own formation first (§7), including the
  assessment redesign that AI-era integrity requires;
- preceptors and clinical faculty are educators under this doctrine
  wherever they form learners, whatever their payroll title.

## 12. Human-in-the-loop lifecycle doctrine

Human oversight binds across the whole lifecycle of any AI that
touches formation, consistent with the American Academy of Nursing's
human-in-the-loop standard:

- **Development:** nurse educators participate in problem definition,
  data curation, and failure-mode analysis before a tool is built or
  bought;
- **Validation:** tools are trialed in realistic simulations with
  educator observers, tested on edge cases and diverse learner
  populations, and must demonstrate that they do not add errors or
  cognitive burden before any learner-facing use;
- **Routine use:** high-impact outputs prompt human review by design;
  educators and learners can override, annotate, or disable the tool,
  with overrides logged; monitoring reviews performance, drift, and
  override patterns on a schedule, and findings feed governance.

The portable policy sentence:

> All AI participating in nurse formation shall operate as a human-in-the-loop system. Educators retain final authority over every summative decision, learners retain authorship of their own formation, and AI outputs must be reviewable, overridable, and traceable. Human overseers must be trained, empowered, and accountable for monitoring AI behavior, intervening when necessary, and escalating concerns through named governance pathways.

## 13. Claims and evidence doctrine

Formation claims follow the same bounded-language discipline as every
public NIN/NAIO artifact. Without an appropriate evaluation design and
named evidence, no formation artifact or program may claim:

- licensure or examination success;
- clinical competence, readiness, or entrustment;
- clinical-hour equivalence or substitution;
- accreditation, certification, or continuing-education standing;
- causal learning improvement;
- faculty replacement or supervision reduction.

Review labels are dimensional — educationally reviewed, specialty
reviewed, localization reviewed, technically tested — never one
generic badge. Popularity, usage minutes, completion counts, and
satisfaction scores must never be represented as evidence of learning.

## 14. Embedding doctrine: Nurse AI OS

### 14.0 Canonical classifications

Formation uses the current vocabularies of the NIN–NAIO Master
Directive v1.1:

- **Risk tiers:** Green, Yellow, Orange, Red-P (prohibited), and Red-E
  (exceptional high-risk under separately governed conditions only).
- **Data classes:** D0 public, formally deidentified, or synthetic; D1
  private personal professional material; D2 confidential
  organizational material; D3 PHI or other sensitive regulated
  material; D4 credentials, secrets, and control-plane assets.
- **Action modes:** Observe, Draft, Recommend, Prepare Action, Act
  With Approval, and Constrained Autonomy. Unrestricted autonomy is
  prohibited.

Learning environments are D0/Green by default; ambiguity narrows
capability and fails closed.

### 14.1 Enforced today (reference implementation evidence)

Several planks of this doctrine already run in this repository's
[Integration Contract](../naio-integrations/). That evidence supports
the direction and activates nothing.

| Doctrine plank | Runtime surface | Mechanism |
|---|---|---|
| Rule 1 — First commitment | Judgment layer | Deterministic commit-then-compare probes on every request; students coach-first by default through the policy engine's own role-alias table |
| Rule 2 — Visible reasoning | Judgment layer + gateway | Reasoning ledger over every output; consequential recommendations without visible assumptions, alternatives, or change conditions are refused with `EDENA-JUDGMENT-VISIBILITY` |
| Rule 3 — The practice field | Healthcare sandbox | Synthetic-only admission boundary (schema allowlist, generation markers, privacy screen); governance unchanged for synthetic data; synthetic passages retrieve last and always carry a warning |
| Rule 4 — Human evaluative authority (protective floor) | Policy engine | Student mode blocks patient-specific recommendations and caps students at Draft (`EDENA-STUDENT-MODE`); side-effect actions require recorded approval |
| §10 — Learner data | Data zones + tenants | Private reflections never reach faculty, manager, or cohort views (`EDENA-PRIVATE-REFLECTION`); no silent zone migration (`EDENA-ZONE-MIGRATION`); tenant isolation fails closed |
| §12 — Traceability | Observability | Every decision traced to a tenant-separated, hash-chained, metadata-only audit stream |
| §6 — Scoped roles | Role packets | Pre-licensure-student and educator packets ship explicit DATA-BOUNDARY records and templates |
| §4.5 — Proof of work | Multi-role identity + Deliverable Studio | The portfolio is a shared store held once and filtered per role lens; Deliverable Studio scaffolds produce never-final outputs a human finishes, owns, and can carry as portfolio evidence |

### 14.2 Planned enforcement (design intentions, not yet implemented)

The following are named so they can be built honestly, in the same
pattern as the research governance gates. None exists until built,
tested, and verified:

- **Summative-intent gates:** `grade_assignment`,
  `progression_determination`, `competency_signoff`, and
  `professionalism_finding` as gated execution intents requiring a
  named human educator approval recorded on the request — proposed
  reason code `EDENA-EDUCATOR-AUTHORITY`;
- **Formation-mode delivery:** probe-before-answer presentation at the
  host surface for coach-first roles, with answer-first as the
  justified exception;
- **Bias-sighting route:** a structured report intent that feeds
  governance review and returns disposition to the reporter;
- **AI-assistance disclosure record:** a per-artifact disclosure
  ledger for coursework, bound to the artifact rather than the
  honor system;
- **The growth mirror:** a learner-owned longitudinal view of
  judgment development (probe engagement, ledger reading, challenge
  quality) living in the private zone and shared only by the
  learner's choice.

## 15. Success doctrine

Formation succeeds when demonstrated clinical judgment grows, learner
agency grows, educator capacity is redirected toward human teaching,
and outcomes are equitable across learner populations.

It is not successful because usage minutes rise, completions
accumulate, or answers arrive faster. **An education AI that optimizes
engagement is optimizing the wrong loop.**

Measures must consider:

- growth in clinical judgment on NCJMM-aligned assessment over time;
- the quality of learner challenges, overrides, and bias sightings;
- depth of the proof-of-work portfolio — outputs actually produced,
  used, and carried forward;
- mentorship time protected or enlarged by AI adoption, and the
  health of community participation;
- educator hours redirected from clerical work to formative contact;
- equity of outcomes across learner subgroups;
- disclosure and integrity health;
- appropriateness of runtime refusals (protection, not obstruction);
- learner and educator burden, trust, and unintended effects.

## 16. Amendment and decision rights

Material changes to evaluative authority, learner data use, summative
gating, claims language, minor participation, surveillance posture, or
institutional scope require explicit founder judgment and the
applicable governance process.

AI may draft amendments and analyze impacts. It may not ratify
doctrine, activate a program, certify a competency, or grant
educational authority.
