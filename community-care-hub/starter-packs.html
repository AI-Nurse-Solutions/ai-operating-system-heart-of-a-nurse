Cause Starter Packs

Nurses don't treat a patient they haven't assessed. A hub shouldn't serve a community it hasn't assessed either. Every pack below follows the nursing process, ADPIE, applied to one cause in one place.

A · Assess the area About 2 weeks

Pick one geographic area first: a neighborhood, a ZIP code, a town, or a county. Smaller is better. Then gather two kinds of information.

What the numbers say

Public data, free to look up:

  • County Health Rankings: your county's health compared with others.
  • U.S. Census data: ages, languages, income, who lives where.
  • Your local hospital's Community Health Needs Assessment. Nonprofit hospitals publish one every three years, usually on their website.
  • 211: your local directory of services. Call 211 or visit your area's 211 website.
  • Your county or city health department's reports.

What people say

Numbers miss what neighbors know:

  • Hold one listening session with 8 to 12 people the cause affects. Ask three questions: What's hardest right now? Where do you go for help? What would make a difference?
  • Talk to three local partners already doing this work. Ask what they can't keep up with.
  • Write down people's words exactly. Quotes are evidence too.

Assessment is done when you can answer: Who is affected here? What help already exists? What's missing or hard to reach?

D · Diagnose the need One 90-minute meeting

1. Write a community diagnosis

Use the same shape as a nursing diagnosis:

[The problem] related to [the cause], as evidenced by [what you found].

Example: "Families missing follow-up care after hospital discharge, related to scattered resources and no transportation, as evidenced by 9 of 12 listening-session members not knowing where to get rides to appointments."

Choose one diagnosis to start. You can add more after your first 90 days.

2. Run a SWOT

Strengths and weaknesses are inside your group, like a patient's own resources. Opportunities and threats are outside, like the patient's environment and support system.

StrengthsInside, helpful

What are we already good at? Who do we know? What do people trust us for?

WeaknessesInside, harmful

Where do we drop the ball? What do we lack: time, people, skills, languages?

OpportunitiesOutside, helpful

Who else wants this solved? What funding, events, or new officials could help?

ThreatsOutside, harmful

What could block us: mistrust, cuts, competing groups, misinformation?

The 45-minute SWOT walkthrough:

  1. 10 minutes, quietly: everyone writes one idea per sticky note, as many as they can.
  2. 10 minutes, sort: place each note in one of the four boxes. Group repeats together.
  3. 10 minutes, vote: each person marks their top 3 in each box.
  4. 15 minutes, connect: a top Strength plus a top Opportunity is what you lead with. A top Weakness plus a top Threat is what you guard against.

3. Write your mission statement

We help [who] in [where] to [change we want] by [how we do it].

Check it three ways: it fits on one sticky note, a neighbor would understand it, and it names who you serve. Then carry it into the Soul Interview.

P · Plan your helpers from what you found

The rule: no helper without a finding. Just as no medication goes on the chart without an indication, every AI helper should trace back to something your assessment or SWOT turned up.

Start with no more than four. Each pack below suggests a starting lineup, but your own findings decide. Then set one 90-day goal with three or four measures.

I and E · Implement and evaluate

  • Implement with the Hub Setup Guide and your signed Charter.
  • Evaluate every month with the Monthly Quality Check. At 90 days, repeat a short assessment and compare.

Pack 1 · Maternal and family health

Who it's forGroups supporting pregnant people, new parents, and young families: doulas, community health workers, faith groups, parenting circles.
AssessLook up: March of Dimes PeriStats for your area, your county's prenatal care and infant health data, local WIC and home-visiting programs. Listen to: recent parents, doulas, clinic staff.
Common diagnosesLate or missed prenatal visits. Families unsure where to get support after birth. Language or transportation barriers to care.
SWOT promptsStrength: trusted messengers already in the community? Opportunity: hospitals or officials focused on maternal health? Threat: misinformation, clinic closures?
Sample mission"We help new and expecting families in [area] get to the care and support they need by connecting them to trusted local services."
Likely helpersResource Navigator (prenatal, WIC, lactation, childcare), Member Welcome & Volunteer, Event & Outreach (classes, support circles), Translation & Access.
Red linesNo pregnancy or medical advice. Anyone describing urgent warning signs, such as heavy bleeding, severe headache, or trouble breathing, is told to call 911 or their provider right away. See the CDC's Hear Her warning signs.
90-day measuresFamilies connected to a service. Referrals that reached a real appointment. Volunteers active.

Pack 2 · Veterans and military families

Who it's forVeterans' groups, military spouse networks, faith communities, and family support organizations.
AssessLook up: nearby VA facilities at VA.gov, your County Veterans Service Officer, Military OneSource for families. Listen to: veterans, spouses, and teens in military families.
Common diagnosesVeterans unaware of benefits they've earned. Families isolated after a move or deployment. Long waits or distance to mental health care.
SWOT promptsStrength: veteran peers who've been through it? Opportunity: local bases, VSOs, faith-based programs? Threat: stigma about asking for help, distrust of systems?
Sample mission"We help veterans and military families in [area] find the benefits and support they've earned by keeping a trusted, up-to-date guide and a friendly person to call."
Likely helpersResource Navigator (benefits, mental health, faith-based support), Member Welcome & Volunteer (peer matching), Event & Outreach, Listening Agent.
Red linesNo counseling and no crisis assessment. Every crisis goes to people right away: the Veterans Crisis Line (dial 988, then press 1) and 911 for emergencies. No benefits decisions: refer to an accredited service officer.
90-day measuresVeterans and families connected to a verified service. Warm hand-offs completed. Peer volunteers active.

Pack 3 · Nurse and healthcare workforce

Who it's forNurse networks, professional chapters, schools of nursing, and healthcare worker groups.
AssessLook up: your state board of nursing's workforce data, HRSA workforce projections, local nursing school enrollment. Listen to: frontline nurses on every shift, new graduates, nurse educators.
Common diagnosesNew nurses leaving within their first year. Frontline concerns that never reach decision-makers. Too few clinical training placements.
SWOT promptsStrength: experienced nurses willing to mentor? Opportunity: staffing committees, new legislation, school partnerships? Threat: burnout, fear of speaking up?
Sample mission"We help nurses in [area] turn what they see at the bedside into organized, evidence-based improvements by listening, gathering, and sharing their voices."
Likely helpersListening Agent (shift-friendly surveys), Member Welcome & Volunteer (mentor matching), Research Scout (evidence and policy updates), Impact Reporter (briefs for leaders).
Red linesNo patient information of any kind. No identifiable staff complaints and no employer-confidential material. Every concern is summarized without names before anyone outside the group sees it.
90-day measuresNurses heard in listening sessions. Mentor pairs formed. Evidence briefs delivered to decision-makers.

Pack 4 · Healthcare access and affordability

Who it's forNeighborhood associations, food banks, libraries, faith groups, and clinics helping people afford and reach care.
AssessLook up: community health centers near you, free coverage help at HealthCare.gov Local Help, your state's Medicaid office, low-cost prescription programs. Listen to: uninsured and underinsured neighbors, front-desk staff at clinics.
Common diagnosesPeople skipping care because of cost. Confusion about coverage and enrollment deadlines. Medications not filled because they cost too much.
SWOT promptsStrength: trusted community spaces like libraries and churches? Opportunity: open enrollment season, health center outreach? Threat: coverage changes, scams targeting people seeking help?
Sample mission"We help neighbors in [area] get the care they can afford by guiding them to coverage help, low-cost clinics, and prescription programs."
Likely helpersResource Navigator (clinics, coverage help, prescription programs), Event & Outreach (enrollment days), Translation & Access, Impact Reporter.
Red linesNo eligibility decisions, no insurance or legal advice, and no collecting income or ID details. Coverage questions go to certified navigators. Warn members never to share Social Security numbers with the hub.
90-day measuresPeople connected to coverage help. Clinic visits scheduled. Enrollment events held.
