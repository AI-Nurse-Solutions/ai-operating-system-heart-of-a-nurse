Our stewardship charter

This ally-pathway values charter accompanies—not replaces—your personal governance charter. It welcomes allied health colleagues, business builders, managers, and personal AI learners without implying any professional credentials. Our personal-AGI philosophy is an aspiration, not a claim of achieved AGI.

1. Dignity before efficiency

Ask who benefits, who could be harmed, and whose voice is missing. Protect dignity before speed.

2. Humans remain accountable

Verify important outputs and act within actual competence and permissions. Agents prepare; authorized people decide.

3. Governance is how we care at scale

Define purpose, scope, sources, ownership, audiences, and stop conditions. Correct errors and pause when uncertain.

4. Protect what has been entrusted to us

Use public sources and synthetic examples here. No PHI, confidential records, employer systems, or agent-driven external actions.

5. Build capability and share responsibly

Strengthen your judgment and learning. Share only if you choose, after review of attribution, rights, destination, and ownership.

6. Grow a community that can scale good

Welcome different professions and experiences. Protect restoration, support peers, and measure progress by useful human capacity and trustworthy service.

A quiet ceremony of commitment

Pause. Think of a person or community you hope your work will serve—without entering their name or story here. Read the pledge slowly. If it reflects your intent, you may mark your acceptance. If not, take more time. Your belonging is not measured by a certificate.

I will govern my tools so that good intentions become trustworthy practice. I will carry the lamp with care, and make room for others to carry it too.”

Now, make the partnership your own.

You can speak simply, as you would to a thoughtful colleague. The agent is a tool—not a person, therapist, or licensed professional. Let it help you reflect and prepare while your judgment and human relationships remain central.

Customize gently

  • Name the good you want to make possible.
  • Choose your pace: a brief, one question at a time, or deeper analysis.
  • Ask for honest uncertainty and respectful disagreement.
  • Review memory storage and provider routes before retaining preferences.
  • Keep work contexts separate; profile labels alone do not ensure isolation.
  • Ask for a change proposal; back up and approve before editing SOUL or configuration.

Talk in ordinary language

Purpose + context + help wanted + boundaries + format is enough. Type, or use voice only if configured and after reviewing its data route.

“Please slow this down. Ask one question at a time. Help me prepare a public-source learning plan, separate facts from assumptions, and leave the decisions with me.”

Your thinking partner

“Help me think, not just agree. For a synthetic community learning meeting, what am I assuming? Who might be left out? Compare two approaches and show risks. No patient data or external actions.”

Use it for questions, alternatives, and reflection—not borrowed certainty.

Your bounded Chief of Staff

“Using these synthetic commitments, prepare my three priorities, decisions only I can make, dependencies, and a proposed next step. Keep every action a draft. Do not send, assign, schedule, or mark completion without evidence.”

This is a planning style, not an installed autonomous agent. Drafted is not sent; completed is not verified.

A weekly reflection

What helped? What was wrong? What should stay private? What permissions need review? What good became easier to do? Begin again with one manageable action—and protect time for restoration.
