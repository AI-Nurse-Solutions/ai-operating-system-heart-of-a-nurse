# 01 — The Lamp and the Ledger

### Why the People Who Can't Afford to Be Wrong Build AI Differently

**Format:** 60 minutes · live or virtual · no software required to attend
**Series:** Nurse AI OS — General Practice Workshops
**Boundary:** Non-clinical. No patient care, no medical advice, no PHI. All examples use
participants' own non-sensitive material.

---

## The promise

> **Setup:** Most AI advice you've heard comes from people whose worst case is a bad quarter.
>
> *(pause — two full seconds)*
>
> **Reveal:** This comes from people whose worst case is a person. And it turns out that when
> you build for the people who can't afford to be wrong, you build the version everyone
> actually wanted.

**One sentence:** You will leave with a working definition of a *governed* AI workspace and
the three habits — own your context, keep the human gate, keep the ledger — that make AI
trustworthy enough to use on the things that actually matter to you.

---

## Who this is for

Anyone who has used AI and quietly stopped, because it was impressive and useless in the
same session. First-time users who feel behind. Professionals whose employers have opinions
about AI and haven't explained them. Nurses who want to use this outside the hospital, on
their own life. Entrepreneurs and owner-operators deciding whether this is a tool or a toy.
No technical background assumed and none acquired.

---

## Learning outcomes

By the end of this session, participants will be able to:

1. Distinguish a **chatbot** (stateless, borrowed, forgetful) from a **workspace** (owned
   context, standing rules, recorded decisions) and explain why the difference shows up in
   the quality of the answers.
2. Name the three governance habits and describe the specific failure that occurs when each
   one is missing.
3. Apply the **cost-of-being-wrong test** to any task, and use it to set how much
   verification that task deserves.
4. Identify, in their own life, one task where AI use is currently risky and one where it is
   already safe.
5. Explain nurse-governed AI — steward, human gate, no sensitive data, the right to refuse —
   in plain language to a colleague, a partner, or a skeptical boss.

## Run of show (60 minutes)

| Time | Segment | What happens |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 0–5 | **Open: the worst answer** | One card each: the most confidently wrong thing an AI ever told you. Read four aloud. Establish that the room already knows the problem. |
| 5–12 | **Teach: chatbot vs. workspace** | Why fluency reads as correctness. Why your best prompt dies when the tab closes. Why "it doesn't remember me" is not a memory problem — it's an ownership problem. |
| 12–17 | **⏸ DEMO PAUSE 1 — Cold start vs. warm start** | *See notes below.* |
| 17–25 | **Teach: the three habits** | Own your context. Keep the human gate. Keep the ledger. For each: one sentence of what it is, one story of what happens without it. |
| 25–30 | **⏸ DEMO PAUSE 2 — The refusal** | *See notes below.* |
| 30–38 | **Exercise: the cost-of-being-wrong grid** | Each participant writes five real tasks on cards and places them on a two-axis grid: *how often I do this* × *what it costs me if it's wrong*. Low-cost + frequent is where you start. High-cost is where the gate lives. |
| 38–43 | **⏸ DEMO PAUSE 3 — The ledger** | *See notes below.* |
| 43–52 | **Pairs: the handover and the never-list** | "One task I'm handing over this week. One I'm never handing over, and why." Partners push on the *why*. Six share out. |
| 52–60 | **Close** | Closing reflection, call to action, and the one-page card everyone leaves with. |

## Demo pause notes

**⏸ DEMO PAUSE 1 — Cold start vs. warm start (≈5 min).**
Ask the same real question twice: once of a blank assistant, once of a workspace that has
already read a context file. The point is not that the second answer is prettier — it's that
the second answer is *about the person asking*. Let the room see the generic answer first and
sit with how familiar it feels.
*Do not show:* anything containing a real name, client, employer, or private detail you
haven't cleared. Use your own material.

**⏸ DEMO PAUSE 2 — The refusal (≈5 min).**
Ask the workspace for something outside its boundary and let the room watch it decline. This
is the single most persuasive demonstration in the entire series, because everyone in the
room has been told AI will do anything you ask. Say out loud: a system that can't say no
isn't safe enough to say yes.
*Do not show:* a staged or fake refusal. If the refusal doesn't work live, say so and move on.

**⏸ DEMO PAUSE 3 — The ledger (≈5 min).**
Open a written decision record from two weeks ago. Show the question, the options, what was
chosen, and what would change the answer. Then reopen it and update it. The reveal is that
the value isn't the AI's output — it's that you can see your own thinking from a fortnight
ago, unedited by hindsight.

---

## Practical takeaways (usable tonight)

- **The three-habit card** — own your context, keep the human gate, keep the ledger — with
  one prompt to start each.
- **The cost-of-being-wrong grid**, filled in with five of their own real tasks.
- **A never-list**: the things they have decided, in writing, not to hand over. Two items is
  enough to start.
- **One standing rule**, written as a single sentence, that they will apply to every AI
  session from tonight (e.g. "It drafts, I send.").
- **A starter context prompt** they can paste into any assistant this evening to begin the
  file that Workshop 02 builds properly.

---

## Closing reflection and call to action

> Nightingale's lasting contribution was not the lamp. It was the ledger — the insistence
> that what happened be written down, counted, and looked at honestly, especially when the
> numbers were unflattering. The lamp is what people remember. The ledger is what changed
> outcomes.
>
> Everyone in this room is about to be handed an extraordinarily capable, extraordinarily
> confident assistant. The lamp part is easy. The question is whether you'll keep the ledger.

**Call to action:** Before you sleep tonight, write your never-list. Two things you will not
hand over, and one sentence each on why. It takes four minutes and it is the difference
between using this tool and being used by it.
