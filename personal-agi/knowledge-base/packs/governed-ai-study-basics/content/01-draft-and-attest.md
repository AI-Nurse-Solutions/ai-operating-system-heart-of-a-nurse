# Draft and attest

The single habit that keeps AI useful and you in charge: **the AI drafts, you attest.** Nothing an AI produces is finished work until a human has read it, judged it, and put their name on it.

## The practice

1. **Scope the ask before you prompt.** Say what the draft is for, who will read it, and what the AI must not do (no fabricated citations, no invented patient details, no clinical claims).
2. **Let it draft — never let it file.** The AI's output lands in front of you, not in your submission, your inbox, or anyone's record. If a tool offers to send, post, or file for you, decline that mode.
3. **Read the whole draft.** Not skim — read. The failure mode that hollows this practice is attesting work you did not actually review.
4. **Judge it against something outside the AI.** Your textbook, your course rubric, your program's policy. The draft is one input, never the arbiter.
5. **Attest or revise.** When you submit it, it is yours — the effort, the errors, and the learning. Record what you changed; the deltas are where you are still learning.

## Commit-then-compare, for study

The strongest study version of draft-and-attest reverses the order: **commit to your own answer first**, then compare it with the AI's. Answer the practice question, write the care-plan section, or explain the mechanism yourself — then ask the AI and study the differences. The gap between your answer and the draft is the most precise map of what to study next that you will ever get.

## Why this matters now

Tools increasingly offer to act, not just draft — to log in, fill forms, and complete screen work for you. Whatever they automate, keep the gate: an agent may gather, prepare, and stage; a human reviews and commits. Never let an agent log in to school or clinical portals on your behalf.

## Limitations

This guide describes a working habit, not your program's academic-integrity policy — your school's rules govern, including its AI-disclosure requirements. It assumes text-based coursework; simulation and clinical settings carry additional rules this guide does not cover.
