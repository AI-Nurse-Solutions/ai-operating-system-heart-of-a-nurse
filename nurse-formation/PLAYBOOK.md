# NIN Nurse Formation Operational Playbook

## 1. Operating purpose

This playbook turns the [NIN Nurse Formation Doctrine](DOCTRINE.md)
into an adoption and operating method for programs, educators,
learners, and preceptors. It covers governance-first adoption, faculty
formation, course integration moves, learner practices, assessment and
integrity workflows, the bias-sighting route, human-in-the-loop
adoption gates, learner data procedures, measures, and explicit
deferrals.

It does not authorize institutional deployment, PHI processing,
competency determination, continuing-education credit, minor
participation, automated proctoring, or autonomous action.

## 2. Governance-first adoption model

Adopt in this order, and only in this order:

```text
Program intent
        ↓
Governance adoption (policies, named humans, stop authority)
        ↓
Faculty formation (literacy before learner exposure)
        ↓
Bounded course pilot (disclosed, formative-only)
        ↓
Supervised operation (override review, term evaluation)
        ↓
Revise, expand, pause, or stop
```

No step automatically grants the next state. A tool purchase is not a
policy; a policy is not faculty readiness; a pilot is not a program.

## 3. Roles and decision rights

| Role | May | May not |
|---|---|---|
| Program lead / dean | Adopt policy, authorize bounded pilots, name accountable humans, stop any use | Delegate summative authority to AI or let procurement precede governance |
| Governance steward | Run adoption gates, triage bias sightings, review overrides, maintain records | Approve their own proposals as independent review |
| Course educator | Integrate AI in disclosed formative roles, judge and sign all summative decisions, run debriefs | Deploy undisclosed AI in the learner's loop or use detector output as sole misconduct evidence |
| Preceptor / mentor | Use AI to organize observation and prepare coaching within scope | Convert AI summaries into evaluations without their own observation |
| Learner | Use disclosed tools, commit first reads, challenge outputs, report bias sightings, appeal | Present required-disclosure AI work as unassisted thinking |
| AI (agents) | Frame, probe, drill, draft for human review, simulate on synthetic data, summarize what the learner may see | Grade alone, gate progression, lead a consequential debrief alone, or monitor covertly |

Where independent review is claimed, the proposer cannot be the only
approver.

## 4. Program adoption playbook (ninety days)

### Days 1–30 — Govern first

Deliver:

- the human-in-the-loop policy (adopt the doctrine §12 policy sentence
  verbatim or stronger);
- the summative-authority policy: **no AI output may be the sole basis
  for a grade, progression, remediation, professionalism, discipline,
  competency, or entrustment decision**;
- the disclosure policy (when learners disclose AI assistance; when
  the program discloses AI to learners — both directions, always);
- the learner data policy (zones, minimal collection, no secondary
  use, correction route);
- the bias-sighting route with a named steward;
- named accountable humans and a named stop authority.

Gate: policies published to learners and faculty; no tool is live yet.

### Days 31–60 — Form the faculty

Deliver:

- foundational AI literacy workshops for participating faculty
  (capabilities, failure modes, bias, privacy, disclosure);
- hands-on practice with the intended tools on synthetic cases only;
- an assessment redesign session: which existing assessments still
  measure thinking in an AI-saturated world, and which must change;
- bias-autopsy training (§5.4).

Gate: named educators complete faculty formation before any learner
exposure. No program may require of learners a literacy its faculty
have not begun.

### Days 61–90 — Bounded pilot

Deliver:

- one course or unit, disclosed to every enrolled learner in writing;
- formative uses only — nothing summative touches AI in the pilot;
- weekly review of overrides, refusals, and bias sightings;
- an end-of-pilot evaluation against the doctrine's success measures.

Gate: evaluation before expansion; the stop authority can end the
pilot at any point without appeal.

## 5. Educator playbook — course moves

### 5.1 The commit-then-compare assignment

The learner records their own read first — noticing, interpretation,
planned response. Then the AI answers with visible reasoning. The
graded artifact is the **comparison**: where the learner and the
machine diverged, which assumptions each carried, and what the learner
now holds and why. A human grades it. This makes AI use the object of
judgment rather than its substitute.

During a Days 61–90 bounded pilot, this assignment runs **ungraded**:
the comparison is written and debriefed as formative work, consistent
with the pilot gate that nothing summative touches AI. The graded
version follows only after the pilot evaluation clears expansion.

### 5.2 AI-visible assignment design

Every assignment states its AI posture up front: prohibited, permitted
with disclosure, or required. Permitted-and-required assignments
collect process evidence (prompts used, what was accepted, what was
rejected and why) as part of the submission, so the thinking stays
visible.

### 5.3 Simulation with a human debrief

AI drafts scenarios from synthetic case bundles and may prepare
debriefing questions and organize observations. The educator leads the
debrief. A consequential debrief is never delegated to the machine —
the debrief is the lesson.

### 5.4 The bias autopsy

Take one real AI output into class and interrogate it together: Who
was in the training data? Who was left out? What context about this
patient or population did it miss? How would it perform for the people
in front of us? The write-up is a bias sighting exercise and feeds the
program's sighting log.

### 5.5 The rubric guardrail

AI may draft rubrics and feedback; the educator judges, edits, and
sends in their own voice. Learners receive educator feedback informed
by AI, never AI feedback wearing an educator's name.

### 5.6 Refusal literacy

Teach what the runtime refusals mean: student mode blocking a
patient-specific recommendation is a protection, not an obstruction;
a refused naked verdict is the system requiring judgment material.
Reading a refusal correctly is applied AI literacy.

## 6. Learner playbook

- **First read first.** Write your commitment — what you notice, what
  you expect, what you would do — before you ask. The comparison is
  where you learn.
- **Read the ledger.** The assumptions, alternatives, and change
  conditions attached to an answer are the lesson; an answer without
  them deserves your suspicion.
- **Disclose by habit,** even when not strictly required. Disclosure
  is a professional reflex you are building for licensure life.
- **Challenge the tool.** When output conflicts with your assessment
  or your patient's context, that conflict is a finding. Record it and
  report it through the bias-sighting route.
- **Know what stays private.** Your reflections, journals, prompts,
  and drafts live in your private zone. Faculty see what you submit,
  not what you wondered.
- **Refusals are teachers.** When the system declines, ask what
  boundary it is holding and why that boundary exists for someone at
  your stage.

## 7. Preceptor, mentor, and practicing-nurse playbook

- **Run the quartet, not a tool.** Any mentorship, residency, or
  development program pairs the AI tutor with a named human mentor,
  an active community circle, and a proof-of-work portfolio from day
  one; a missing support is a program gap to close, not a feature to
  defer.
- Transition-to-practice programs use the same rules: commit before
  compare, synthetic practice fields, human evaluative authority over
  every progression decision.
- **Community circles:** peer groups meet to review work-in-progress
  on synthetic material, normalize struggle, and surface bias
  sightings together. AI may schedule circles and summarize what
  participants choose to share; it never attends as a silent
  evaluator.
- **The growth mirror stays the learner's.** Mentors see the mirror
  when the learner shares it; a mentoring conversation about the
  mirror is coaching, never an evaluation record.
- Run **override review huddles**: cases where a nurse overrode,
  corrected, or distrusted an AI are reviewed as learning material.
  Overrides and near misses are learning signals, not user failure.
- Practicing nurses climb the same literacy ladder (foundational →
  applied → stewardship) through continuing development; stewardship
  literacy feeds the pipeline of nurse governance participants.
- Preceptors document their own observations; AI summaries may prompt
  a conversation but never substitute for observed practice.

## 8. Assessment procedures

| Activity | AI may | AI may not | Human requirement |
|---|---|---|---|
| Practice / formative feedback | Generate, adapt, and score where validated, disclosed, and appealable | Report results to anyone but the learner without disclosure | Educator reviews patterns, not every item |
| Item and rubric development | Draft for review | Deploy without human review | Educator edits and approves each item |
| Summative scoring | Assist where validated, disclosed, appealable | Be the sole basis of any grade | Educator judges and signs |
| Progression / remediation / discipline | Organize evidence a human will weigh | Decide, recommend-as-default, or rank learners | Named human decision with appeal route |
| Integrity investigation | Surface process evidence | Serve as sole evidence (AI detectors are unreliable) | Human process: conversation, evidence, appeal |

Item-writing workflow: AI drafts → educator reviews and edits → peer
or psychometric check where stakes warrant → versioned bank entry with
AI-assistance disclosure → deployment by human decision only.

Integrity procedure: begin with a process conversation, not an
accusation; collect process evidence; no AI-detector output may be the
sole basis for a misconduct finding; prefer educational response where
the failure is formative; preserve appeal.

Proof-of-work portfolio procedure: every learning arc produces at
least one actual output — a deliverable, project artifact, or
synthetic-case write-up — entered in the learner's portfolio with
date, context, and AI-assistance disclosure. Scores and module
completions annotate portfolio entries; they never substitute for
them. The learner owns the portfolio and chooses what mentors and
programs see beyond what policy requires.

## 9. Bias-sighting procedure

1. **Notice** — output conflicts with professional judgment, misses
   context, or performs differently across populations.
2. **Record** — structured note: tool, output, population or context
   concern, what the human judgment held instead.
3. **Route** — to the named governance steward; the reporter gets an
   acknowledgment.
4. **Review** — steward triages; patterns escalate to tool review,
   vendor questions, or retirement.
5. **Close the loop** — the reporter learns the disposition; sightings
   aggregate into a term report that faculty and learners can see.

A sighting is a contribution, never a complaint on the reporter's
record.

## 10. HITL adoption gates for any education AI tool

### Gate A — Adopt

- stated formation purpose (which judgment muscle does it load?);
- human-in-the-loop conformance against doctrine §12;
- learner data zone fit and minimal collection;
- subgroup performance evidence requested from the vendor, and the
  absence of it recorded as a risk;
- disclosure plan for learners and faculty;
- exit plan (how the program leaves this tool).

### Gate B — Validate

- educator simulation trial on synthetic cases;
- edge cases and diverse learner populations tested;
- no-added-burden check: the tool must not add educator documentation
  burden or **extraneous** learner cognitive load in controlled trial —
  the productive effort of commitment and comparison is the design,
  not a defect, so this gate screens for wasted load, never for the
  deliberate friction the doctrine prescribes;
- pilot consent language ready.

### Gate C — Operate

- review prompts live on high-impact outputs;
- override, annotate, and disable routes tested and logged;
- monitoring schedule set (performance, drift, override patterns);
- term review on the calendar before the first learner logs in.

No gate may be skipped because a tool is popular, free, or already
licensed elsewhere in the institution.

## 11. Learner data procedures

- collect the minimum; assign every record an explicit zone at
  creation;
- private reflections, prompts, drafts, and retrieval history stay in
  the private zone — no faculty, manager, cohort, or vendor view;
- cross-zone moves require explicit, recorded approval;
- retention is bounded and stated; correction routes are published;
- analytics are purpose-limited, disclosed, and aggregate by default;
- no learner data leaves for model training, product improvement, or
  sale without separate explicit informed consent that names the use.

## 12. Measures

Measure:

- clinical judgment growth on NCJMM-aligned assessments over time;
- quality and disposition of learner challenges, overrides, and bias
  sightings;
- depth and use of the proof-of-work portfolio — outputs produced,
  carried forward, and shown;
- mentorship time protected or enlarged, and community-circle
  participation;
- educator hours redirected from clerical work to formative contact;
- equity of outcomes across learner subgroups;
- disclosure health and integrity-process fairness;
- refusal appropriateness and burden signals.

Do not infer or claim from AI use alone:

- NCLEX, licensure or examination success;
- clinical competence, readiness, or entrustment;
- clinical-hour equivalence;
- accreditation, certification, or CE standing;
- causal learning improvement without an appropriate evaluation
  design;
- faculty replacement or supervision reduction.

## 13. Explicit deferrals

Do not include in any first implementation:

- minors or pre-college programs;
- automated proctoring, gaze tracking, or emotion inference;
- automated remediation assignment;
- competency, readiness, or entrustment scoring;
- clinical-hour substitution claims;
- patient-facing teaching tools;
- continuing-education credit issuance;
- LMS gradebook write-back;
- PHI or D3/D4 processing;
- summative-intent gates represented as live before they are built
  and verified.

## 14. Adoption decision register

The following require explicit named human judgment before
implementation or public claim:

- program scope, pilot course, and stop authority;
- tool selection and vendor terms;
- disclosure language shown to learners;
- assessment redesign decisions;
- learner data retention periods;
- expansion beyond the bounded pilot;
- any claim made to accreditors, boards, or the public.

## 15. Release gate

Before any formation capability is called published, operational,
validated, approved, or authorized, record: exact commit and candidate
digest, applicable doctrine sections, EDENA/data/action scope, test
results, independent review status, unresolved findings, deployment
state, live verification evidence, authority and limitations, and
rollback and incident contacts.

Designed, documented, implemented, tested, committed, merged,
deployed, and live-verified are distinct states.
