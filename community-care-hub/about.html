About Us

The Community Care Hub was built by nurses who believe that good doesn't scale on its own. It has to be organized, guided, and cared for on purpose.

Robert Domondon, RN, CCRN, CSC, CMC

Founder, Nurse Intelligence Network

I've worked night shifts in the ICU for more than twenty years. At 3 a.m., I'm the one coordinating everyone around a patient: the family, the physicians, the respiratory therapist, the pharmacist, and the social worker who'll help them get home. Most of the time, the help a family needs already exists. It's just scattered. A big part of nursing is connecting those pieces.

Before my years at the bedside, I trained as a physician in the Philippines and served as hospital administrator of a former U.S. Naval Hospital. I've also spent years in community work, and I've seen how much good gets lost when energy outruns organization.

The Community Care Hub brings those worlds together. It takes the way nurses coordinate care and puts it in the hands of the community leaders who are already doing the work.

Patty Hsu, BSN, RN, CCRN

Co-founder, Community Care Hub · Empowerment Ambassador, Nurse Intelligence Network

I spent my career in critical care nursing — thirty-one years, including ten years leading a high-acuity ICU and an open-heart unit in a small community hospital. What I keep coming back to is quieter than a rescue story: people do not change because they are told to. They move when someone finally makes room for them to exist.

That is the work I do in the Community Care Hub — the human side of it. A community already knows what it is carrying. What it often lacks is a place to put it down, and a rhythm for picking it back up together. My part is encouraging community leaders to channel their own vision and mission: to own their hub, choose their AI, and guide the person who stays connected to the work and approves everything the system drafts. What they build with it — a charity, a nonprofit, a neighbourhood project — is theirs to keep, whether they are in a city with every resource or a town with almost none.

People keep the judgment. Anyone in crisis goes to a person. And if a community cannot say where its data lives, it is not ready — I would rather say that out loud than let momentum decide.

Nurse Intelligence Network (NIN)

The Nurse Intelligence Network, LLC is a nurse-led organization working to make sure AI in health and community care stays safe, understandable, and in human hands.

We believe nurses shouldn't just receive technology built without them. We should help design it, guide it, and decide when it's ready. NIN brings nurses and community partners together to build tools like the Community Care Hub, and to train people to use AI with care and good judgment.

What we stand for:

  • Patient safety and human dignity, always.
  • Care before speed. We'd rather release something safe than something fast.
  • Community as the owner. The people served should hold the controls.
  • Honesty about limits. Every tool we make says clearly what it won't do.

How we sustain our work: NIN offers training and certification programs. We don't sell the Community Care Hub or anyone's data.

NAIO Institute (planned)

The NAIO Institute is a planned nurse-led nonprofit. Its purpose is to hold tools like the Community Care Hub in the public interest for the long term, and to set clear, nurse-led standards for using AI safely in health and community settings.

Our plan is for the Community Care Hub to move to the NAIO Institute once it's established, so the hub belongs to the public, not to any one company.
