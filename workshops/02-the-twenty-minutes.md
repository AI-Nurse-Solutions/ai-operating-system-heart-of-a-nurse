# 02 — The Twenty Minutes That Fix Every AI Conversation You'll Ever Have

**Format:** 60 minutes · live or virtual · bring something to write with
**Series:** Nurse AI OS — General Practice Workshops
**Boundary:** Non-clinical. No patient care, no medical advice, no PHI. Participants write
about themselves and their own work only.

---

## The promise

> **Setup:** Almost everyone trying to get better results out of AI is working on their
> prompts. Better wording. Longer instructions. A prompt library someone sold them.
>
> *(pause)*
>
> **Reveal:** The prompt was never the problem. The thing has no idea who you are — and you
> can fix that once, in about twenty minutes, permanently.

**One sentence:** You will write the first real draft of your own context file — the document
that tells any AI who you are, what you're carrying, and how you want to be helped — and
watch generic advice turn into advice that's actually about you.

---

## Who this is for

Anyone who has thought "this would be great if it just knew my situation." Professionals
repeating the same background paragraph into a chat box every single time. Nurses,
freelancers, students, and owners whose lives don't fit a template. Especially good for
people who found Workshop 01 convincing and want the first concrete artifact.

---

## Learning outcomes

By the end of this session, participants will be able to:

1. Explain why **owned context beats prompt-craft**, and why the effect compounds while
   clever prompting doesn't.
2. Draft a personal context file using the four-block structure: *who I am, what I'm
   carrying, how I decide, what I don't want.*
3. Separate **durable** context (values, constraints, how they work) from **disposable**
   context (this week's task), and put each in the right place.
4. Define **spheres** — personal, professional, community — and explain what must never cross
   between them.
5. State clearly what never goes into a context file, and why the file is more useful for
   the omissions than the inclusions.
6. Set a maintenance rhythm so the file stays true instead of quietly going stale.

## Run of show (60 minutes)

| Time | Segment | What happens |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 0–5 | **Open: thirty seconds** | Turn to a neighbour and describe yourself to a stranger who has to help you today. Thirty seconds each. Most people flounder. That flounder *is* the gap you've been prompting around. |
| 5–12 | **Teach: the four blocks** | Who I am · What I'm carrying · How I decide · What I don't want. Why the fourth block does the most work and gets written last by nearly everyone. |
| 12–17 | **⏸ DEMO PAUSE 1 — Same ask, two contexts** | *See notes below.* |
| 17–30 | **Write: guided drafting** | Three minutes per block, timer visible, silence enforced. Facilitator reads two or three prompting questions per block and then stops talking. This is the core of the hour; protect it. |
| 30–35 | **⏸ DEMO PAUSE 2 — Interview me for the gaps** | *See notes below.* |
| 35–42 | **Teach: spheres** | Why one file becomes three. Personal, professional, community. What each sphere is allowed to know. Why a clean wall between them is a feature and not an inconvenience. |
| 42–47 | **⏸ DEMO PAUSE 3 — Switching spheres** | *See notes below.* |
| 47–55 | **Pair test: the helpful stranger** | Swap files. Read your partner's aloud, back to them. Ask one question: *what would a helpful stranger still need to know?* Add it. |
| 55–60 | **Close** | The maintenance rhythm — ten minutes a month — then the reflection and call to action. |

## Demo pause notes

**⏸ DEMO PAUSE 1 — Same ask, two contexts (≈5 min).**
Take one genuinely ordinary request — "help me plan my week", "draft a reply to this",
"what should I do about X" — and run it against no context, then against a context file. Read
the first answer aloud in a flat voice. It will sound like a magazine. Then read the second.
*Do not show:* a context file containing anything you wouldn't read aloud to this room. Model
the boundary while you demonstrate the feature.

**⏸ DEMO PAUSE 2 — Interview me for the gaps (≈5 min).**
Paste a deliberately thin context file in and ask the workspace to interview you for what's
missing — to ask the five questions it would need answered to be genuinely useful. Answer two
of them live. This reframes the file from homework into a conversation, which is what gets
people to actually finish it.

**⏸ DEMO PAUSE 3 — Switching spheres (≈5 min).**
Ask the same question in a personal sphere and a professional sphere and show the posture
change. Then show what the professional sphere *cannot see*. The line to say out loud: your
employer's context and your family's context should never have met, and in most tools they
already have.

---

## Practical takeaways (usable tonight)

- **A first-draft context file** — imperfect, theirs, already better than every prompt they've
  written.
- **The four-block template** on one page, reusable for each sphere.
- **The never-include list**: credentials, other people's private information, anything
  covered by an agreement they've signed, anything they'd not want read back to them.
- **The "interview me for gaps" prompt**, which finishes the file without discipline.
- **A ten-minute monthly review**, entered into their calendar before they leave the room.

---

## Closing reflection and call to action

> You have spent your entire life accumulating context. What you're good at. What you've
> survived. How you like to be told bad news. Who depends on you. What you will not do for
> money.
>
> Every single time you open a blank chat window, you throw all of it away and start again as
> a stranger.
>
> Twenty minutes. Once. And then you stop being a stranger to your own tools.

**Call to action:** Finish the file within 48 hours — not perfectly, just finished — and then
use it for one real task and notice what changed. Bring the difference to the next session.
