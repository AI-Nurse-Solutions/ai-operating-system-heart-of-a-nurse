# 05 — Nothing You Can't Take Back

### Using AI Without Giving Yourself Away

**Format:** 60 minutes · live or virtual · no accounts, no uploads, no sign-ups during session
**Series:** Nurse AI OS — General Practice Workshops
**Boundary:** Non-clinical. No patient care, no medical advice, no PHI, and no healthcare
compliance instruction. This is about *your own* private information — contracts, finances,
family matters, unpublished work, client details.

---

## The promise

> **Setup:** Everyone tells you to be careful what you put into AI. Nobody tells you the rule.
> So you either paste everything and feel vaguely uneasy, or paste nothing and get nothing
> useful.
>
> *(pause)*
>
> **Reveal:** Here is the rule, and it's one sentence: never send anything you couldn't survive
> seeing on the screen behind you. Once you have that, you can stop worrying and start working.

**One sentence:** You will leave with a written personal data boundary — a three-colour card
naming exactly what you'll share, what you'll redact, and what you'll never send — plus the
working habits that keep your material yours.

---

## Who this is for

Freelancers and consultants handling other people's business. Small-business owners with
client lists, pricing, and contracts. Professionals whose employer has an AI policy nobody
has explained. Nurses using AI for non-clinical work who need a boundary they can state in one
breath. Writers and creators worried about unpublished work. Anyone who has hesitated over the
paste button and then done it anyway.

---

## Learning outcomes

By the end of this session, participants will be able to:

1. Classify their own information by **exposure cost** using three questions: who can see it,
   how long does it live, what happens if it leaks or is wrong.
2. Produce a **personal boundary card** with green, amber, and red categories, specific enough
   to act on at speed.
3. Use **redaction and synthetic stand-ins** to get equally good output from de-identified
   inputs.
4. Explain **local-first** working and why "on my machine" is a meaningfully different
   arrangement from "in a chat window".
5. Apply the **portability test** — if this tool vanished tomorrow, what do I still have? — to
   any tool they use.
6. State their boundary in one paragraph to a client, a partner, or an employer, without
   sounding either paranoid or naïve.

## Demo pause notes

**⏸ DEMO PAUSE 1 — Redaction in practice (≈5 min).**
Run the same real task twice: once with actual names, figures, and identifying details, once
with stand-ins — Client A, £X, a role instead of a person. Put the two outputs side by side.
They will be nearly identical in quality. That is the entire argument, and it lands harder
shown than said.
*Do not show:* the un-redacted version on screen. Describe it, then show only the redacted
run. Practise the discipline while teaching it.

**⏸ DEMO PAUSE 2 — Working locally (≈5 min).**
Show files living on your own machine, a workspace reading them without uploading them, and a
complete export. Then delete something and show it gone. People believe "you own your data"
only when they watch something get deleted.

**⏸ DEMO PAUSE 3 — The gate and the refusal (≈5 min).**
Show the system declining a request that crosses its boundary, and show a step that requires
your explicit approval before anything leaves the machine. Say it plainly: nothing sends
itself. The most important safety feature in any workspace is a stop that you control.

---

## Practical takeaways (usable tonight)

- **The boundary card** — green, amber, red — written in their own words and specific enough
  to use at speed.
- **The stand-in technique**, practised once, so the redaction reflex exists before it's needed.
- **The portability test**, applied to every tool currently in their stack.
- **One habit changed today**, not resolved for later.
- **A one-paragraph statement** they can send to a client or employer describing exactly how
  they use AI and what they never put into it. This wins work; that is not the reason to do
  it, but it is true.

---

## Closing reflection and call to action

> Privacy is not secrecy. Secrecy is having something to hide. Privacy is deciding, yourself,
> who gets to know what — and it is one of the few forms of power that costs nothing to
> exercise and everything to recover once you've given it away.
>
> You are not being asked to be afraid of these tools. You are being asked to be the one who
> decides.

**Call to action:** Print the boundary card and put it where you actually work — beside the
screen, not in a folder. Then send the one-paragraph statement to one client, one colleague,
or one family member this week. Saying it out loud is what makes it real.

Boundary: no PHI, no patient-specific decisions, no medical advice, no substitute for
