Read alongside the installation video

The video's “sterile field” is a useful metaphor—not a security or compliance guarantee. Some technical claims require correction:

  • Local Hermes does not mean offline inference. Cloud models, tools, memory services, and integrations may send data outside your device; review all routes.
  • A workspace folder is not a sandbox. Selecting a folder alone does not prove that an agent cannot access other files. Separate permissions and tested controls are required.
  • SOUL readback is not enforced governance. A prompt acknowledgment does not bind an agent to a professional license, verify credentials, or demonstrate runtime enforcement.
  • file:// does not prove network isolation. A local HTML file can still contact external services depending on its code.
  • The equipment example is not a current hardware specification. Check official Hermes requirements; local LLM hardware needs vary.
  • Local files do not guarantee exclusive ownership or privacy. Provider terms, accounts, sync, backups, retention, employer rights, and permissions still matter.

No PHI, private customer or employee records, or confidential workplace data is authorized here. Use the current ally setup guide and official Hermes documentation, preserve existing configuration, and obtain exact-scope approval before changes.

Why the community and ecosystem matter

Other systems may be more capable for a particular task. Compare them honestly. Choosing this approach is about the practices and people you want around your work—not proof that our AI is superior, safer in every situation, or guaranteed to deliver outcomes.

Community: learn with others

Different professions and lived experiences can expose blind spots, support correction, and make learning less lonely. This is an invitation to mutual support, not a claim of an active membership program. Browsing, downloading, or accepting a charter does not enroll you anywhere.

Ecosystem: connect purpose to practice

Public prompts, guides, and governance ideas provide a common language for preparing work and reviewing risk. They are not all installed capabilities, tested integrations, or enforced safeguards. Start with the parts you can actually inspect and use.

Heart: keep the person visible

Ask who benefits, who might be harmed, and whose voice is missing. Protect time for rest and human connection. Useful work matters more than impressive output volume.

Data control: choose intentionally

Keep your own reviewed drafts, minimize what you share, and check every destination. Ownership aspirations do not override provider terms, employer rights, sync, backups, or retention. Local setup is not automatic sovereignty.

Positive choices are commitments—not a score

  • Choose purpose over hype: name a concrete good you hope to support.
  • Choose responsibility: verify important outputs and own your decisions.
  • Choose intentional permissions: approve only an exact, understood scope.
  • Choose data control: minimize information and inspect routes and retention.
  • Choose learning: seek evidence, welcome correction, and practice.
  • Choose contribution: share only if you wish, with review, attribution, and named ownership.

These are self-declared intentions, not proof of ethical conduct, assessed competence, or eligibility. Private use is welcome; contribution and ceremony are optional.

A place for your real responsibilities

Allied health and service

Organize public-source learning and prepare non-patient-specific discussions. Respect your own professional scope; no nursing identity or clinical authorization is conferred.

Starting a business

Compare a service idea, draft plain-language customer information, and list claims requiring evidence. No guaranteed income, market advantage, or business success.

Managing ethically

Prepare synthetic agendas and questions about fairness, accessibility, and consequences. An assistant cannot supply employment authority or decide people's futures.

Personal AI and community

Plan a public-source learning project, reflect on priorities, and prepare a reviewed resource. Protect private life and keep human relationships central.
