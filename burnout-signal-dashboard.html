Span-of-Control & Burnout Signal Dashboard

See the warning signs before you lose a nurse — not in an exit interview. Enter weekly unit totals for three workload patterns research links to burnout-driven turnover: late departures, break-skipping, and PTO non-use. The dashboard describes the trend in plain language. You interpret every flag; the tool never recommends an action.

This dashboard lives entirely in your browser. Nothing is uploaded, no account, no tracking — entries stay in this browser's local storage until you export or clear them. It is aggregate-only by design: the form takes unit-level counts and has no free-text fields, so no name, ID, or per-person record can be entered. As everywhere here: no patient data, no clinical decisions, ever.
Observe mode only. This tool retrieves what you entered, aggregates it, and describes patterns (Directive §20 — Observe). It does not score, rank, predict, or recommend anything about any person or any action. It is not a workforce-management, performance, or surveillance instrument, and it must never be used to identify or evaluate an individual. Runs at EDENA Green: self-entered, unit-aggregate data only — no institutional timekeeping connector is part of this build.

4 · How to read a flag (interpretation rules)

Reused from the Directive's §21A interpretation rules for aggregate wellbeing indicators. These rules are part of the tool, not fine print.

  1. Patterns, not people. Every number here is a unit-level workload pattern. Nothing on this page supports a conclusion about any individual, and the tool refuses to draw trends below the minimum staff count for exactly that reason.
  2. A rising indicator is a prompt to look, not a verdict. It says "the workload pattern changed" — it does not say why, and it does not say what to do.
  3. A falling indicator is an ambiguous signal, not automatically a success. Check for underreporting first: staff who stop logging missed breaks are not staff who started taking them. Read a fall as "verify," never as "celebrate."
  4. The tool describes; the manager interprets. It never recommends an action. What, if anything, to do next is your judgment, taken through your normal leadership and HR channels — outside this tool.
  5. Association is not prediction. These indicators are associated with burnout-driven turnover in published research; they do not predict or diagnose it for your unit, and this dashboard is explicitly not a predictive or diagnostic instrument.

5 · Evidence basis

The three indicator categories — late departures (working past scheduled shift end), break-skipping (missed or interrupted meal breaks), and PTO non-use — follow the nurse-manager workload indicator set described by Laudio Insights with the American Organization for Nursing Leadership (AONL) in their joint report series on nurse manager leadership and span of control, which links these workload patterns and manager span of control to burnout-driven turnover risk. That research is an association, not a causal or predictive claim, and this tracker inherits that limit. Any local association between these indicators and later turnover on your unit should be reviewed quarterly as a non-causal observation — success here is measured by whether managers find the view useful, not by prediction accuracy.

6 · Export and portability

Your data, your workspace. Exports are files your browser downloads to you — nothing is sent anywhere.

The dashboard-summary export follows the Mission Control leader packet template (Measures included · What changed · Limitations · Suggested attention) and is born a draft with a mandatory review banner — a named, accountable human must review and approve it before it is used anywhere.
