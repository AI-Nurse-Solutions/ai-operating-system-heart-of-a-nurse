Messaging Guide

One page so co-founders, partners, and volunteers describe the Community Care Hub the same way. Use these lines as written, or close to it.

The core line

"Nurses already know downtime procedures. The Community Care Hub brings that discipline to your organization: your members, your resources, and your plans, in your hands, ready when systems fail."

In one sentence: a free, open-source, nurse-designed system that helps a community network welcome members, coordinate a cause, connect neighbors to trusted local help, and show its impact, with a person approving everything the AI drafts.

Eight messages, and how to say them

MessageSay it like thisBoundary
1. Ready for the season of glitches"When the EHR goes down, we don't stop caring. We go to downtime procedures. Your organization needs the same: a printed resource list, a backup you can carry, and a hub that can run on your own computer."About your organization's own work. Never a hospital's clinical systems.
2. A backup to old systems"Spreadsheets, someone's personal email, a group chat. One lost laptop or one volunteer stepping away, and the member list is gone. The hub keeps it in one place you own, backed up nightly, with a restore test every month."It organizes your group's information. It doesn't replace any official system.
3. Ready for AI-era attacks"AI makes fake emails and cloned voices cheap. The hub's rule helps: nothing goes out unless a named person approves it, and anything about money, health, or safety goes to a leader."Say "built with good security habits." It's not a security product.
4. Self-sustaining and sovereign"With a local AI on your own computer, plus battery or solar backup, your plans stay in your hands even when the grid or the internet goes down."It still needs power. Never say "outage-proof."
5. Private, and lighter on data centers"With a local AI, your data stays on your computer and everyday work adds no load to a distant data center. A small model on hardware you already own keeps the footprint light."Never say "uses less energy than the cloud." Per task, it doesn't.
6. Practice AI governance, not just talk about it"The ANA called for nurse-led AI guardrails. Here, members practice them on a real system: approvals, green-yellow-red rules, and monthly checks."Don't imply ANA endorsement.
7. Keep the volunteers you recruit"Forty people sign up, six stay. The hub welcomes every new member, gives them a role, and follows up the same week, so your leaders spend their time with people."Describe what it does, not results. No pilot has finished yet.
8. A voice in policy"Nurses now serve in Congress and state legislatures. A network that turns bedside concerns into organized evidence gets heard."Nonpartisan. Never tie it to a party or campaign.

Never say

"Replaces your EHR" or "downtime system" "Protects patient data" (it holds none) "Prevents cyberattacks" "Outage-proof" "Uses less energy than the cloud" "NIST certified" Any pilot results (none yet) "Endorsed by" any office or association
