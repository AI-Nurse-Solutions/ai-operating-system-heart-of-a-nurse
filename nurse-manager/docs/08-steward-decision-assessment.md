## How to read a verdict

- **Go:** recommended direction; implement and verify its stated conditions.
  It does not mean the feature is already safe to release.
- **No go:** reject this behavior within the present personal-app scope.
- **Wait:** a useful possibility needs specific evidence, access, or authority.
  The resume condition is stated so it does not become an indefinite backlog.

The overall recommendation is **Go for a local, personal management pilot**:
one assistant drafting under human review, with useful no-AI workflows.
**Wait for release to real users** until signing, install testing, the audit
prerequisites, and applicable pilot authorization are satisfied. More agents,
connectors, or cloud services do not by themselves demonstrate more value.

| I-3: people fields | **Go** for “me” and a controlled set of role labels in the personal pilot | Reduces accidental naming and makes safer defaults easy. | I-3: concern / containment / preservation hold | **Wait** for accepted definitions | A concern may be recorded without implying institutional incident handling. | I-4: approval in the app | **Go** for explicit human approval of the exact local export, and reject/revoke controls | Makes the app usable without terminal commands. Keep sending, uploading, publishing, and external deletion blocked. Requires the ADR addendum. |
| I-4: evidence packet export | **Go** through the governed local-export boundary | Evidence can reveal activity even without document text. | I-5: kept notes sent to a model again | **Go** only through explicit selection and the existing reviewed preview | Memory otherwise becomes an invisible second source of disclosure and stale advice. | I-5: pack-section drafting | **Go** for one bounded section using permitted project records | Useful drafting can be added without more autonomous agents. State unknowns, retain citations and the AI label, and require human acceptance. No unsupervised submission or organizational decisions. |
| I-5: routine versus substantial AI changes | **Go** for versioning every behavior-governing change; treat added data, tools, effects, or autonomy as substantial | Cosmetic wording can be routine after checks. Prompt changes that alter refusals or output meaning need behavior review; “wording only” is not a reliable exemption. Narrowing can also break useful workflows. |
| I-5: owner approval as independent review | **No go** when independent human review is required | A person approving an assistant's suggestion is human review, but is not a second independent human reviewer. Requirements must say which kind of review is needed; do not silently weaken the stronger one. |
| I-6: naming and visual design | **Go** for “Nurse AI OS Manager,” a home named “Today,” “Policy check” in ordinary screens, and technical names in Help | Plain labels help managers understand the product without learning the architecture. | I-7: measurement | **Go** for local, voluntary pilot evaluation; **No go** for automatic sharing of usage counts or unsupported savings claims | Count completed outcomes and corrections, and learn whether managers and reviewers find the artifact useful. | I-7: security facts distribution | **Go** for preparing factual materials for pilot managers and their designated IT/security reviewers | Distinguish verified behavior from limitations and planned controls. | N-1: EDENA approval checks | **Go** for requiring the specific, valid approval that the requesting actor actually holds | An invented approval or an unrelated approval must not unlock a gate. This tightens authorization but changes the shared Integration Contract, so compatibility tests and the public substantial-change process apply. It does not authorize organization features. |

## Delegation and follow-through

The assistant can choose routine implementation details, build reversible
prototypes, research options, write proposals, and produce test evidence
under the user's coding authorization. It should bring a concise decision
brief when an action changes cost, external access, data use, or authority.

`GOVERNANCE.md` §3 requires: “Substantial changes (architecture, public
interfaces, data boundaries, anything touching `SAFETY.md` posture):
proposal issue, open comment period, steward decision recorded in the
issue.” The Go recommendations now have the steward's explicit approval
for implementation. Public proposal/issue records and ADR publication remain
to be synchronized; this document does not assert that happened.
The assistant must not invent a completed comment period,
account enrollment, external consent, or legal authority.

Each consequential decision should record: the exact choice, who has
authority, the expected benefit, the failure it risks, what remains
blocked, the verification needed before release, and when to revisit it.
Separate permission to build from permission to deploy or disclose data.

Recommended priority: settle I-3's narrow data scope and I-2's audit
design, then I-4's local approval surface. In parallel, prepare the signing
and real-laptop checklist. Keep cloud selection, JEV, Hermes integration,
and organizational expansion waiting for their named evidence and inputs.
