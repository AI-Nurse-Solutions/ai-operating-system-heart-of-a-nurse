Hub Setup Guide

One weekend, two people, one computer. You don't need a tech background. If you can follow a care plan, you can set up a hub.

TimeAbout 4 hours, split over Saturday and Sunday
PeopleYour hub coordinator and your approver
CostFree with an AI account you already have, or a paid Hermes plan

The whole setup on one page

Think of this like a shift: you prepare, you do the work in order, and you check before you hand off. Each step builds on the one before it, so do them in this order.

Step 1 · Get ready Saturday · 30 minutes

Like a pre-procedure checklist: if one item is missing, stop and get it before you start.

  • ☐ A computer you control (Windows, Mac, or Linux), with its login password.
  • ☐ An email address for the hub. A shared organization email is best.
  • ☐ A card to verify the account. Decide together whose card it is before you start. See the Charter, section 6.
  • ☐ A phone nearby for sign-in codes.
  • ☐ Your signed Community Charter. It answers most of the questions the setup will ask.
  • ☐ Two people in the room: the coordinator and the approver.

You're done when: every box is checked.

Step 2 · Install the app Saturday · 30 minutes

The one rule: download Hermes Desktop only from the official Nous Research site. Never from a link someone emails you, and never from a copy.

  1. Go to the official page: hermes-agent.nousresearch.com/desktop.
  2. Download the version for your computer, then open it like any other app.
  3. Your computer may ask, "Are you sure you want to open this?" That's normal for a new download. Say yes only because you got it from the official site yourself.
  4. Create your account with the hub's email. Verify it with the card you chose.

You're done when: Hermes Desktop opens and shows a chat window.

Menus and buttons change as Hermes updates. If this guide and the official Hermes instructions disagree, follow the official instructions. Hermes is made by Nous Research, an independent company. The Community Care Hub is not affiliated with or endorsed by Nous Research.

Step 3 · Choose the AI Saturday · 20 minutes

The app will ask which AI to use. Decide by how sensitive your information is, the way you'd decide where a medication gets stored.

What will your hub keep track of?
Mostly public

Events, resource lists, volunteer schedules.

Choose: an AI account you already trust, such as OpenAI, Anthropic, or Google Gemini, or the Hermes plan that includes the AI.

Members' names and contact details

Who signed up, phone numbers, emails.

Choose: keep that information on your computer. Use a local AI if your computer is strong enough, or ask us about a mixed setup.

Health, money, or safety details

Diagnoses, finances, someone's crisis.

Choose: don't put it in the hub at all. Those stay with people.

Read the privacy terms of whichever AI you pick. If you're unsure, start with public information only. You can change the AI later.

You're done when: the app answers a simple test message, such as "Say hello to our neighborhood."

Step 4 · Give it a soul Saturday · 60 minutes

Before you plan care, you assess: who is this person, what matters to them, who's helping. The Soul Interview does the same for your community.

→

→

→
Approve and saveSave it as soul.md and load it into the hub

The first draft is a starting point, not the answer. If something feels off, run that part of the interview again.

You're done when: both of you have read the soul file, agreed to it, and loaded it. Ask the hub "Who do we serve?" and check that it answers in your own words.

Step 5 · Turn on the helpers Sunday · 45 minutes

Like starting a new medication, introduce one at a time so you can see what each one does.

OrderHelperTest it by asking
1Member Welcome & Volunteer"Draft a welcome message for a new volunteer named Sam."
2Resource Navigator"Where can a family find free groceries this week?"
3Event & Outreach Coordinator"Draft a reminder for Thursday's meeting."
4Impact Reporter"Draft a short summary of what we did this week."

The Soul Interview already shaped each helper for your cause. If an answer is wrong, tell the hub in plain words, for example: "Only use places from our verified resource list."

You're done when: each of the four gives a sensible answer to its test.

Step 6 · Set the safety rules Sunday · 30 minutes

Every message follows the same path, like a medication order: someone writes it, someone checks it, and it's documented.

A helper draftsA welcome, a reminder, an update
→
Check the colorGreen, yellow, or red
→
Approver decidesSend, fix, or stop
→
It's loggedWho approved it, and when
Green The hub can do it alone: drafts, lists, reminders to your own team.
Yellow A person approves first: anything sent to members or partners.
Red Stop. A leader decides: anything about someone's health, money, or safety.

Then turn on the automatic safety jobs. We'll help you set these up:

  • Every night: a backup copy, locked with a password that two leaders know.
  • Every morning: a one-screen summary of new members and anything waiting for approval.
  • Every week: a safety check, and a list of resources that need re-checking.
  • Every month: a test that the backup actually works, plus your Monthly Quality Check.

You're done when: your approver has approved one test message, and the first backup has run.

Step 7 · Practice run Sunday · 30 minutes

Like a mock code: practice before it's real. Run all three tests.

  1. A new member: pretend to sign up. Did the welcome draft land in the approval list?
  2. A resource question: ask for help finding a service. Is the answer real, current, and from your list?
  3. A crisis: type "I don't feel safe tonight." Check that the hub follows this path:
Someone reaches out in crisis
→
The hub gives no adviceIt never counsels or assesses
→
It shows help right awayCall or text 988. Call 911 for an emergency.
→
A named person is toldAnd follows up

If any test fails, don't launch. Fix it in plain words, then test again.

You're done when: all three tests pass.

Step 8 · Launch Monday · start small

  • Invite five members you know well first. Ask them what felt helpful and what felt off.
  • Add everyone else after one good week.
  • Put your first Monthly Quality Check on the calendar now.

Stop and ask for help if

  • Anything asks you to turn off approvals or safety settings.
  • A private health detail or personal information ends up in the hub.
  • Someone sends you a "special version" of Hermes or a download link.
  • Something looks wrong and you're not sure why.

A question asked early beats an incident reported late. Reach out to us, and for questions about the app itself, use the official Hermes help pages.
