# 30-Day Community Organizer Playbook

## When to Use
- A nurse or other organizer is starting to build a community on a unit, council, or project and asks where to begin.
- The organizer wants the day-by-day plan, the five listening questions, the huddle agenda, invitation wording, or the day-30 dashboard.
- Use this **before** deploying the agent team. The first month validates the community, not the technology.

## Procedure
1. Ask which day or week the organizer is in, then give only that part of the plan below.
2. Coach; do not do the relationship work for them. The 1:1 conversations, invitations, and huddles are human work.
3. Help with drafts (purpose statement, invitation, "You said → We did → What's next") in the organizer's own words, and return them for the organizer to send.
4. Track the organizer dashboard measures weekly if asked. Record interest, not just complaints.
5. Do not record judgments about individual colleagues.

## Pitfalls
- Building a platform before anyone else owns anything. If nobody else owns a piece by day 30, a website was built, not a community.
- Selecting people because of titles rather than trust and follow-through.
- Announcing a solution ("we're going to build X") instead of testing whether the themes match people's experience.
- Tracking twenty measures instead of two to four.

## Verification
By day 30: 20–25 conversations, at least two shifts represented, 3–5 recurring issues, one priority selected, one small pilot, and **at least two people besides the organizer owning something**.

---

Make your first 30 days deliberately **relationship-first and low-burden**. On a unit with rotating shifts and existing councils and committees, the goal is not to create another committee. It is to discover what people care about, connect the right people, and finish Day 30 with **one community-owned issue ready for a small pilot**.
# 30-Day Community-Organizing Action Plan
### 30-day objective
By Day 30, aim to have:

- **20–25 meaningful 1:1 conversations**
- Representation from day/night shift and different experience levels

- **3–5 recurring issues** identified
- **5–8 potential community leaders/champions**

- One shared priority selected
- One **small 30–60 day pilot**

- Clear ownership spread among several people, rather than centered on you

## Week 1: Listen
**Goal: Understand the community before trying to organize it.**

| Day | Action | Deliverable |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Write a simple purpose statement | 2–3 sentences |
| 2 | Map the community: frontline nurses, charge nurses, educators, unit councils and committees, quality, leadership | Stakeholder map |
| 3 | Identify 15–25 people representing different perspectives and shifts | Listening list |
| 4 | Conduct first 3–4 conversations | Notes |
| 5 | Conduct another 3–4 | Notes |
| 6 | Speak with night/weekend staff | Broader representation |
| 7 | Review what you're hearing without deciding the solution yet | Initial themes |
### Use the same five questions
Keep conversations informal, around **10–15 minutes**.

1. **What is working well that we should protect?**
2. **What makes your work unnecessarily difficult?**

3. **What is something people keep talking about but doesn't seem to improve?**
4. **If we could fix one thing in the next 90 days, what would you choose?**

5. **Who else should I talk with?**

Add one particularly important organizing question:

**“Would you be interested in helping if a small group worked on this?”**

Record **interest**, not just complaints.

# Week 2: Find the common ground
**Goal: Turn individual conversations into a shared agenda.**
### Days 8–10
Continue conversations until you've spoken with roughly **15 people**.

Start tagging responses:

- Communication
- Education

- Workflow
- Professional development

- Shared governance
- Resources

- Technology
- Scheduling

- Recognition
- Quality/safety
### Day 11: Create an issue map
Example:

| Issue | People mentioning it | Importance | Feasibility | People willing to help |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Finding resources | 9 | High | High | 4 |
| Project visibility | 8 | High | High | 5 |
| Education access | 7 | High | Medium | 3 |
| Scheduling | 6 | High | Low | 2 |
Numbers here are illustrative. Use your actual listening results.
### Days 12–13
Talk with another **5–10 people**, specifically testing whether the themes you've identified resonate.

Don't say:

“We're going to build X.”

Ask:

“Several people have mentioned difficulty finding resources and knowing what's happening with projects. Does that match your experience?”
### Day 14
Reduce the findings to **3–5 community priorities**.

# Week 3: Connect people
**Goal: Move from “one person heard this” to “we're working on this.”**
### Day 15: Identify your core organizers
Look for **5–8 people** who:

- care about the issue,
- are trusted by peers,

- represent different shifts or perspectives,
- actually follow through,

- and are willing to contribute.

Don't select people simply because they have titles.
### Days 16–17: Personal invitations
Avoid a generic mass email initially.

Say something like:

“I've been talking with nurses about what would make our work and shared-governance efforts easier. The same few issues keep coming up, and you were someone I thought could bring an important perspective. I'm bringing together a small group to see whether there's something practical we could improve. Would you be interested?”
### Day 18: Hold your first organizing huddle
Keep it to approximately **30 minutes**.

**Agenda**

**5 min:** Why we're here **10 min:** What we heard **10 min:** Which issue should we tackle? **5 min:** Who wants to help with what?

Your job is to facilitate, not present a finished solution.
### Days 19–21
Have the group select **one issue**.

Use four criteria:

**Impact × Feasibility × Community interest × Ability to demonstrate progress**

# Week 4: Move from conversation to action
**Goal: Produce one visible, achievable first win.**
### Day 22: Define the challenge
Write one sentence:

**“We heard ______ from ______, resulting in ______. During the next 30–60 days, we will test ______.”**

For example:

Nurses reported difficulty knowing what shared-governance projects are underway and how they can participate. We'll test a simple centralized project view accessible to staff.

### Day 23: Define success
Choose **2–4 measures**, not 20.

For example:

- 25 staff members access the resource
- 10 provide feedback

- 5 actively contribute
- ≥80% of respondents say it is easier to find the information

- At least 2 people besides you take ownership of components

### Day 24: Divide ownership
This is extremely important.

Instead of:

**One organizer → everything**

Create something like:

| Role | Responsibility |
| --- | --- |
| Organizer | Facilitation |
| Communications lead | Updates |
| Frontline champion | Staff feedback |
| Night-shift champion | Off-shift representation |
| Project lead | Pilot |
| Documentation lead | Decisions/results |
You can coordinate the system without owning every task.

### Days 25–26: Build the smallest useful version
Resist building a large platform.

For example, if the problem is project visibility, start with:

**One shared list**

| Project | Purpose | Owner | Status | Next milestone | Help needed |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
Then post it where staff already look: the team channel or the community home page.

You don't initially need a dashboard, automated workflows, an AI agent, or a large application.

**Prove the community behavior before scaling the technology.**

## Day 27: Invite participation
Now communicate more broadly:

**You spoke. We listened.**

Over the past few weeks, nurses shared several opportunities to improve how we work together. One recurring theme was ______.

A small group of colleagues is testing a simple approach to improve it.

**Here's what we're testing:** ______ **Here's how you can help:** ______ **Time required:** 5–10 minutes **We'll report back:** ______

Notice the framing.

Not:

“I created…”

But:

**“We heard…”**

# Day 28: Recognize people
Recognition is part of organizing.

Highlight:

- who contributed,
- what they contributed,

- which shift/team they represent,
- and what changed because of their involvement.

Don't wait for the project to be completed.

Recognize participation.

# Day 29: Report back
Produce a simple:
## YOU SAID → WE DID → WHAT'S NEXT
**You said**

Three recurring themes emerged.

**We did**

A small frontline group selected one issue and designed a pilot.

**Initial result**

What happened so far.

**What's next**

How staff can participate during the next 30 days.

This creates credibility because people can see that speaking up resulted in action.

# Day 30: Community reflection
Bring the core group together for **30 minutes**.

Ask only four questions:

1. What worked?
2. What didn't?

3. Who else should we involve?
4. What should we do during the next 30 days?

Most importantly:

**Who is willing to lead part of the next phase?**

That question begins transforming participants into organizers.

# Your simple operating system
You could manage the entire first month with a very lightweight structure:

```
Feedback form
Listening + ideas
       ↓
Shared list
Issues + people + projects
       ↓
Team channel
Conversation + coordination
       ↓
Community home page
Visible community home
```
I would **not build dashboards or sophisticated automation during these first 30 days** unless volume genuinely requires it. Your first month should validate the *community*, not the technology.

# Your organizer dashboard
Track these weekly:

| Measure | Day-30 target |
| --- | --- |
| 1:1 conversations | **20–25** |
| Different shifts represented | **≥2** |
| Recurring issues found | **3–5** |
| People expressing interest | **10+** |
| Core organizers | **5–8** |
| Community priority selected | **1** |
| Pilot launched/planned | **1** |
| New leaders taking ownership | **≥2** |
The most meaningful metric is the last one.

If after 30 days you have built a beautiful community site but nobody else owns anything, you built a **website**.

If 5–8 nurses are saying **“here's what we're working on next,”** you've started building a **community**.
### The 30-day progression
**Week 1: LISTEN** ↓ **Week 2: DISCOVER COMMON GROUND** ↓ **Week 3: CONNECT & ORGANIZE** ↓ **Week 4: ACT & SHOW PROGRESS** ↓ **Day 30: DEVELOP THE NEXT LEADERS**
