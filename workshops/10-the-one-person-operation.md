# 10 — The One-Person Operation

### Running a Real Business With a Governed AI Workspace

**Format:** 60 minutes · live or virtual · bring your actual offer and your actual pipeline
**Series:** Nurse AI OS — General Practice Workshops
**Boundary:** Non-clinical. No patient care, no medical advice, no PHI, no clinical services.
Education, coaching, consulting, writing, products, services, trades, creative work.

---

## The promise

> **Setup:** The advantage of being small used to be that you could move fast. That advantage is
> gone; everyone moves fast now, and most of them have more people than you.
>
> *(pause)*
>
> **Reveal:** The advantage now is that you can hold the entire business in one place — one
> context, one memory, one set of standards — and be the only person who has to. That is a thing
> no company of forty can do at all.

**One sentence:** You will leave with the four working parts of a one-person back office — offer,
pipeline, delivery, and the weekly review that keeps all three honest — set up in a workspace
that already knows your business.

---

## Who this is for

Solo founders and owner-operators. Freelancers and consultants. Nurses and other professionals
building a non-clinical business on the side — courses, coaching, writing, products, services.
Small-business owners with one or two staff and no back office. People who are excellent at the
work and losing money on everything that surrounds it.

---

## Learning outcomes

By the end of this session, participants will be able to:

1. State their **offer in one sentence** that a stranger can repeat back correctly — the actual
   test, and one most businesses fail in the room.
2. Build a **business context file**: offer, pricing, ideal client, boundaries, voice, what they
   don't do.
3. Diagnose the real failure point in solo businesses — **follow-through, not marketing** — and
   build a pipeline they can maintain in fifteen minutes a week.
4. Do **market and competitor research from public sources**, with sources shown and
   unverifiable claims flagged rather than asserted.
5. Draft outreach and follow-ups that are **specific rather than mass-personalised**, and say
   why the difference is commercially decisive.
6. Set a **client-data boundary** and state it in one paragraph a client can read.
7. Run a **thirty-minute weekly business review** on six fixed questions.

## Demo pause notes

**⏸ DEMO PAUSE 1 — The workspace that knows the business (≈5 min).**
Show a business context file — offer, pricing, ideal client, what you don't do, your voice —
and then ask for something ordinary: a reply to an enquiry, a scoping note, a rough proposal.
The reveal is that it comes back priced correctly and sounding like the owner, because it was
told the things the owner knows and never writes down.
*Do not show:* real client names, real contract terms, or anything from a live negotiation. Use
your own business or a de-identified composite.

**⏸ DEMO PAUSE 2 — Research without a research budget (≈5 min).**
Research a market, a competitor, or a customer segment from public sources — with sources shown
and claims it cannot verify explicitly flagged. Spend as much of the demo on the flagged claims
as on the findings. A confident, unsourced market summary is worse than no market summary,
because you'll price against it.

**⏸ DEMO PAUSE 3 — Specific beats personalised (≈5 min).**
Draft three real follow-ups to three real conversations, then edit one by hand in front of the
room and show the difference. Say it plainly: mass personalisation is transparent and everyone
resents it. Specificity — you remembered the thing they actually said — is not scalable, and
that's exactly why it works.

---

## Practical takeaways (usable tonight)

- **A one-sentence offer** that survived being repeated back by two strangers.
- **A first-draft business context file.**
- **A pipeline list** where every live conversation has a next date and a next action.
- **Three drafted follow-ups**, human-edited, ready to send.
- **The client-data paragraph**, usable in proposals and on their website.
- **Six weekly-review questions** and a calendar slot: what came in · what moved · what went
  quiet · what did I promise · what am I owed · what's the one thing next week.

## Closing reflection and call to action

> Nobody has ever hired a business. They hire the person who answered — the one who came back
> when they said they would, remembered what was said, and did the thing.
>
> Every tool in this workshop exists to protect your capacity to be that person, on the
> fortieth week of a hard year, when you're tired and the admin has piled up and the easiest
> thing in the world would be to let one more conversation quietly end.

**Call to action:** Send one follow-up before you leave this room. Not tonight — now, from the
list you just wrote. The one you've been avoiding for eleven days. Then run your first weekly
review inside seven days.
