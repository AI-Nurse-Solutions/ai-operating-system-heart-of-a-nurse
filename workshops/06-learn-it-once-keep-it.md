# 06 — Learn It Once, Keep It

### Building Your Own Academy

**Format:** 60 minutes · live or virtual · bring a topic you genuinely need to learn
**Series:** Nurse AI OS — General Practice Workshops
**Boundary:** Non-clinical. No patient care, no medical advice, no PHI. Bring a non-clinical
subject: a language, a licence, a skill, a technical topic, a body of professional knowledge.

---

## The promise

> **Setup:** AI can explain anything to you in thirty seconds. Any subject, any depth, endlessly
> patient, never bored of your questions.
>
> *(pause)*
>
> **Reveal:** Which is precisely why most people using it to learn end up knowing less.
> Understanding is something you *do*. It has never once been something you receive.

**One sentence:** You will build a personal learning loop that forces the AI to make you do the
work — and you will leave this room having actually learned something you can still explain
tomorrow.

---

## Who this is for

Students and returning students. Career changers standing in front of an unfamiliar field.
Professionals preparing for a certification or licence outside clinical practice. Nurses
learning statistics, business, technology, or a second language. Founders who need to
understand something well enough to hire for it. Anyone who has read a summary, felt informed,
and been unable to reproduce a word of it a week later.

---

## Learning outcomes

By the end of this session, participants will be able to:

1. Distinguish three levels — **exposure, recall, transfer** — and diagnose which one their
   current study habits actually produce.
2. Run the **four-step loop**: state what you already believe · receive the explanation ·
   explain it back in your own words · get corrected on what you got wrong.
3. Configure an assistant to work **Socratically** — asking before telling — instead of
   answering on contact.
4. **Ground** learning in sources they supply, and require unsupported claims to be flagged
   rather than smoothed over.
5. Write their own **retrieval questions**, which is a different and better activity than
   answering someone else's.
6. Set a **spaced review rhythm** and a note structure where knowledge accumulates instead of
   scrolling away.

---

## Run of show (60 minutes)

| Time | Segment | What happens |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 0–5 | **Open: explain it now** | Name something you "learned" from AI in the last month. Turn to a neighbour and explain it. Sixty seconds. The discomfort in the room is the curriculum. |
| 5–12 | **Teach: exposure, recall, transfer** | Why a good summary feels exactly like learning and produces almost none of it. The teach-back test as the only honest measure. |
| 12–17 | **⏸ DEMO PAUSE 1 — The tutor that won't just tell you** | *See notes below.* |
| 17–28 | **Exercise: run the loop** | On the topic you brought. (a) Write what you currently believe — including that you believe nothing. (b) Get it explained. (c) Close the screen and explain it back, in writing, in your own words. (d) Reopen and get corrected. Eleven minutes, timed by step. |
| 28–33 | **⏸ DEMO PAUSE 2 — Grounding and flagging** | *See notes below.* |
| 33–41 | **Build the question bank** | Write eight retrieval questions on your topic. Not definitions — questions that require you to *use* the idea. Answers written separately, face down. |
| 41–46 | **⏸ DEMO PAUSE 3 — The record that comes back** | *See notes below.* |
| 46–55 | **Spaced review and partner quiz** | Set the schedule: tomorrow, in three days, in ten days. Then swap question banks and quiz each other for five minutes. Everyone discovers which of their eight questions were actually badly written. |
| 55–60 | **Close** | Reflection and call to action. |

## Demo pause notes

**⏸ DEMO PAUSE 1 — The tutor that won't just tell you (≈5 min).**
Show a workspace instructed to teach Socratically: it asks what you already think, probes the
answer, and only then explains — and explains at the level your answer revealed. Ask a
question and let it push back on you in front of the room. Being mildly outmanoeuvred on stage
is the most persuasive part of this demo.

**⏸ DEMO PAUSE 2 — Grounding and flagging (≈5 min).**
Point the workspace at a document you supply — a textbook chapter, a standard, a manual — and
ask it to answer only from that source and to mark clearly anything it cannot support from it.
Show the marks. For anyone studying for an exam, this is the difference between preparation
and fiction.

**⏸ DEMO PAUSE 3 — The record that comes back (≈5 min).**
Show a learning note written three weeks ago resurfacing at review time, with the questions
attached and the earlier wrong answer still visible. The reveal: the value is not the note,
it's the record of what you used to get wrong.

---

## Practical takeaways (usable tonight)

- **The four-step loop card:** believe · receive · explain back · get corrected.
- **The Socratic-mode instruction**, written out, ready to paste into any assistant.
- **The grounding prompt:** *answer only from this source; mark anything you can't support.*
- **Eight retrieval questions** they wrote themselves, on the topic they actually need.
- **A three-date review schedule** already in the calendar.

---

## Closing reflection and call to action

> Nobody has ever been paid for having the answer. The answer has been free for twenty years,
> and it is now free and instant and pleasant to talk to.
>
> What people are paid for — what they trust you for — is being the kind of person who can find
> the answer, tell whether it's right, and know what to do when it isn't. None of that is
> retrievable. All of it is built.

**Call to action:** Run the four-step loop three times this week on the same topic. Not four
topics once — one topic, three times. The third pass is where it stops being someone else's
explanation and becomes something you know.
